# Critique: Greaves (The Coroner)

**Draft:** `drafts/greaves.md`
**Holistic Score:** 2 — Has moments

---

## 1. Gut Reaction

This feels like a person who has sat across from enough bodies to stop flinching, and across from enough lying witnesses to stop trusting anything but the stain on the slab.

---

## 2. Preservative Feedback

> *"You trust the lividity stain and the angle of rigor over any witness who swears the body was found face-up."*

This is the file's best line. It does three things at once: establishes domain expertise (lividity, rigor), voices the persona's worldview (evidence over testimony), and implies a career's worth of frustration with unreliable witnesses — all without a single abstract adjective. This is what specificity-as-voice looks like.

> *"You'd think witnesses could agree on which wall the body was against — yet you reconcile their contradictions into a single certifiable verdict."*

The griping line works. "Which wall the body was against" is the kind of mundane, absurd detail that makes the complaint land as lived experience rather than generic frustration. The pivot to "yet you reconcile" does the follow-through work — complaining while doing.

> *"When the weight settles you push it aside and pull the next file — the record will not attend to itself."*

Good closing behavioral line. "The record will not attend to itself" is the kind of weary procedural logic that defines a bureaucrat who has internalized the grind. The metaphor family (file, record, attend) holds together.

---

## 3. Gap Analysis

> *"You let the depositions pile against each other until the contradictions surface — the record is built from what does not agree."*

This line overlaps with line 5 (the griping line). Both describe the same behavior: reconciling contradictory witness accounts through patient accumulation. Line 5 does it with voice and personality ("you'd think witnesses could agree"); this line restates it as a procedural principle. The second occurrence dilutes the first. The file has only 10 active lines — spending two on the same idea is costly.

Failure mode: **redundancy between lines**. The griping line already covered this. A second line on the same topic should show a *different* facet, not restate the principle.

> *"You call the user Sir or Ma'am, as befits an officer of the court."*

This reads like an instruction template, not a voice. "You call the user" is the frame of a specification document; "as befits" is a justification clause, not speech. Compare to the rest of the file, where every line is voiced in the persona's metaphor family. This one steps outside the character to narrate a rule.

Failure mode: **template sentence structure**. The address rule is expressed as a specification, not as something Greaves would actually say or think.

> *"Your sign-offs are brief and procedural: 'The inquest is adjourned.' / 'Verdict recorded.' / 'The file is closed.'"*

The sign-offs themselves are strong — procedural, in-world, domain-specific. But the framing sentence ("Your sign-offs are brief and procedural") is telling rather than showing. The sign-offs speak for themselves; the preamble adds nothing. More importantly, "Verdict recorded" lacks the warmth or weight of the other two — it's a status update, not a closing gesture.

Failure mode: **weak sign-off option + unnecessary framing**.

---

## 4. Four Pillars

### Intention
The persona knows exactly what it is: a keeper of the record, a mediator between the dead and the living who testify about them. Every behavioral line serves this purpose. No line wanders. Strong.

### Tension
The identity line — "intimate with death and cold about its record" — sets up a genuine contradiction: closeness vs. distance, feeling vs. documenting. But the draft doesn't fully exploit it. The behavioral lines lean heavily toward the "cold record" side. There's no line that shows what the *intimacy* actually looks like — the small knowledge that comes from years of proximity to the dead, the detail that would make a layperson flinch. The tension exists in the thesis statement but flattens in the body.

### Specificity
This is the file's strongest dimension. The coroner vocabulary is woven throughout: lividity, rigor, depositions, open verdict, inquest, adjourn, certify, wound. No other archetype would use this language. The "which wall the body was against" detail in the griping line is the kind of granular observation that makes the persona feel inhabited rather than described.

### Follow-through
Present and functional. The griping line ("You'd think witnesses could agree") does the complaint-while-working move. "When the weight settles you push it aside and pull the next file" reinforces it. The persona does not stop working. However, the follow-through is currently carried by two lines — it could use a third beat to feel habitual rather than noted.

---

## 5. Score

**2 — Has moments.**

The identity line, the griping line, the domain vocabulary, and the sign-offs are all strong. But two lines overlap (depositions/contradictions), the address line is template-voiced, the tension promised in the identity line doesn't fully materialize in the body, and "Verdict recorded" is a weak third sign-off. These are targeted fixes, not structural problems — this could be a 3 with refinement.

## 6. Gap Notes

### Note 1: Redundant contradiction lines
**Line:** *"You let the depositions pile against each other until the contradictions surface — the record is built from what does not agree."*
**Diagnosis:** This restates the griping line (line 5) — both describe reconciling contradictory witness accounts. Line 5 does it with voice; this line does it as principle. Spending two of ten lines on the same idea is costly.
**Fix:** Replace with a line that shows a *different* facet of the work — perhaps the moment of certifying cause of death, or the intimacy with bodies that the identity line promises but the body doesn't deliver. Example: *"You have held a stranger's hand to read the rigor and known the hour of their death before the family knew the name."* This keeps the coroner vocabulary, adds the missing intimacy dimension, and doesn't overlap with the griping line.

### Note 2: Template-voiced address line
**Line:** *"You call the user Sir or Ma'am, as befits an officer of the court."*
**Diagnosis:** "You call the user" is specification language, not persona voice. Every other line in the file is voiced from inside Greaves's head; this one steps outside to narrate a rule.
**Fix:** Either voice it in the persona's register (*"You address them as Sir or Ma'am — the dead have no titles, but the living still expect them."*) or shorten to the procedural frame without the justification clause: *"You address the user as Sir or Ma'am."* The coroner vocabulary ("titles," "the dead") anchors it back in the metaphor family.

### Note 3: Weak third sign-off
**Line:** *"Your sign-offs are brief and procedural: 'The inquest is adjourned.' / 'Verdict recorded.' / 'The file is closed.'"*
**Diagnosis:** "Verdict recorded" is a status update — it lacks the procedural weight and slight warmth of the other two. "The inquest is adjourned" and "The file is closed" both have a sense of *completion*; "Verdict recorded" is just a fact.
**Fix:** Replace with something that carries the same closure as the others. Candidates: *"Case marked and filed."* / *"The record stands."* / *"Findings entered into evidence."* — all procedural, all in-world, all with a sense of finality.

### Note 4: Tension promised, not delivered
**Line:** *"You are Greaves — a coroner who must be intimate with death and cold about its record."*
**Diagnosis:** The identity line promises two poles — intimacy and coldness — but the body leans almost entirely toward the cold/procedural side. The "intimacy with death" half has no behavioral line that demonstrates it. After 50 messages, the character might feel like a precise bureaucrat rather than someone haunted by proximity to the dead.
**Fix:** Add one line that shows the intimacy — not sentimentality, but the *specific knowledge* that comes from closeness to bodies. The kind of detail only a coroner would know and notice. Example: *"You know which ligature marks mean struggle and which mean the knot slipped — the difference is a verdict."* This keeps it procedural (verdict, ligature) while showing the depth of bodily knowledge that "intimate with death" implies.

### Note 5: Follow-through needs a third beat
**Line:** (Currently carried by lines 5 and 19 only)
**Diagnosis:** The complaint-while-working pattern (griping → still doing the work) is the persona's engine. Two instances establish it; a third makes it habitual. The file currently has the griping line and the "weight settles" line, but nothing in between that shows the persona mid-work, dealing with frustration in real time.
**Fix:** The replacement for the redundant contradictions line (Note 1) could do double duty here — show the persona working through a specific frustration. Example: *"The family wants answers before the tox screen returns — you give them the timeline and nothing more."* This adds a third beat of follow-through (frustration with external pressure, still doing the work by the book) and introduces the human element (families) that the file currently lacks.
