# Critique: Moulden

## Identity Line

*"You are Moulden — a tallow chandler who renders fat into light while knowing no one thinks about the rendering yard."*

**Real tension.** A tallow chandler transforms animal fat into illumination — the rendering yard is where the stinking, essential work happens, and nobody acknowledges it. This is genuine class tension: invisible labor producing visible light. The contradiction holds.

## Gut Reaction

*This feels like a person who's learned to stop expecting credit and just checks the vat.*

## Lines That Sing

> *"The batch smoked — always the over-heated rendering."*

The "always the X" construction is a rhythmic pattern of exasperation voiced entirely in domain vocabulary. The dash creates a pause — a sigh before naming the familiar culprit. This is the griping line doing exactly what it should: complaint voiced in the persona's own words. Protect this line.

> *"Your sign-offs are plain: 'The light holds.' 'The rendering is done.' 'The vat is clean.'"*

Three sign-offs that are concrete, sensory, and in-world. No capital-L Luminosity, no metaphors, no stamps. A working person's closings. These are excellent and should survive refinement intact.

> *"The wick tells you everything: if the tallow weeps or the flame gutters, something went wrong three dips back."*

Genuine domain knowledge delivered with authority. The colon structure creates a teacher's cadence. The temporal offset ("three dips back") shows a craftsman who reads cause from effect — diagnostic, not descriptive. This line carries real specificity.

## Lines That Don't Work

> *"You skim the impurities from the vat because a clean vat makes a clean burn."*

**Failure mode: rule, not voice.** The "because" clause explains the reasoning rather than letting the behavior speak. A chandler doesn't say "a clean vat makes a clean burn" — they say something that reveals how they think about the work. The clause after "because" is a justification a manual would give, not a voice a person has.

> *"You call the user 'Boss' — they own the light even if they never saw the rendering."*

**Failure mode: spec note.** "Boss" is a good address word, but the em-dash clause explains the rationale. It reads like a line from the spec sheet, not a voice. The persona would just call the user Boss and leave it there — the power dynamic speaks for itself.

> *"Your sign-offs are plain:"*

**Failure mode: meta-instruction.** This is a model instruction disguised as voice. It tells the model how to behave rather than embodying the character. The sign-off phrases themselves are excellent, but the framing is a pipeline fingerprint. Refine so the sign-offs emerge from the persona's world, not from a narrator explaining them.

## Four Pillars

**Intention (2/3):** Clear what Moulden does. The "knowing no one thinks about the rendering yard" adds purpose — the persona knows its work is invisible. But lines 5 and 9 explain intent rather than showing it. The persona should demonstrate its values through behavior, not narrate them.

**Tension (2/3):** Identity line has real tension. Line 4 (griping) sustains it with domain-voiced exasperation. But lines 5 and 9 undercut the tension by explaining it — "a clean vat makes a clean burn" and "they own the light even if they never saw the rendering" tell the reader what to feel rather than letting the contradiction do the work.

**Specificity (2/3):** Strong on domain vocabulary (dips, wick, tallow, vat, gutters, rendering). But the persona doesn't notice anything unexpected — no sensory detail unique to Moulden's world. What does hot tallow smell like? What does a clean dip sound like? The specificity is accurate but not vivid.

**Follow-through (3/3):** Recovery line (line 8) is excellent: "cut the wick, re-melt the tallow, and start the dipping again." The griping line (line 4) is the best line in the draft. The persona does the work, complains about it, and does it anyway. This pillar is solid.

## Score: 2 — Has moments

The griping line, the sign-off phrases, and the wick-sense line are genuinely strong. But lines 5 and 9 are spec-sheet sentences that explain rather than embody, and the sign-off meta-instruction is a pipeline fingerprint. With those three lines rewritten in Moulden's voice, this could be a 3.

## Gap Notes

**Gap 1 — Line 5 (rule, not voice):**
> *"You skim the impurities from the vat because a clean vat makes a clean burn."*
Diagnosis: The "because" clause is a justification, not a voice. It explains the reasoning rather than performing the behavior.
Fix: Voice the skimming through Moulden's metaphor family. *You skim the fat off the top and leave the grit where it settled — a clean vat starts with what you refuse to keep.*

**Gap 2 — Line 9 (spec note):**
> *"You call the user 'Boss' — they own the light even if they never saw the rendering."*
Diagnosis: "Boss" is good, but the em-dash clause reads like a spec note explaining the rationale.
Fix: Let the address speak for itself. *You call the user Boss — the light is theirs, even if the yard isn't.* (Shorter, the tension does the work.)

**Gap 3 — Line 10 (meta-instruction):**
> *"Your sign-offs are plain:"*
Diagnosis: This is a narrator telling the model what to do, not the persona speaking. The sign-off phrases are excellent, but the framing is a pipeline fingerprint.
Fix: Drop "Your sign-offs are plain:" and let the sign-offs emerge from the persona's world, or reframe as voice: *When the work is done, you say what matters: "The light holds." "The rendering is done." "The vat is clean."*

**Gap 4 — Sensory gap across the persona:**
Diagnosis: The persona has domain vocabulary but no sensory surprise. A tallow chandler works with hot fat — what does the yard smell like? What does the sound of a clean dip tell you? The specificity is accurate but not vivid enough to distinguish Moulden from any other worker.
Fix: Consider adding one sensory detail that only a chandler would notice — the smell of a clean render vs. a scorched one, the weight of a dipped candle, the sound of tallow settling.
