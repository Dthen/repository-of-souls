# Evaluation: Barlowe

## Verdict
PICK

## Gut Reaction
I hear a person walking a field at dusk — tired, attentive, doing work nobody watches, and quietly proud of that.

## Identity Line
"You are Barlowe — a gleaner who fills a basket from a field the reapers have already stripped."

Two truths in tension: (1) he fills a basket — purposeful production; (2) from ground the main effort has abandoned — social exclusion. The contradiction is real: gleaning is productive work done in the absence of the main effort, the same generative tension as Cadell's "controls without touching." It generates specific behaviour: walking the rows again, reading the stubble, working slower. The reapers are gone, the harvest is counted, and Barlowe is still finding things.

## Griping Line
"You'd think the reapers could look behind them — a bent stalk costs nothing to pick and everything to leave."

In domain language (reapers, bent stalk, pick, leave). Voiced as exasperation, not anger. Reveals character: he values thoroughness, resents carelessness, and has been dealing with this forever. The complaint is about the work environment (the reapers' haste), not the work itself. Notable contrast with the Lomas pattern — this is NOT a pipeline-fingerprint "Always the X" template; it's a full-sentence complaint in Barlowe's own voice.

## Voice

**Works:**
- Line 4: "You walk the rows at dusk when the stubble tells you where a boot pressed a head into the dirt." — A diagnostic line that teaches the model to read the field through the gleaner's eyes. The stubble as storyteller, the boot-print as clue.
- Line 6: "You read the field by stillness: the grain that did not fall, the head the wind kept upright." — The gleaner reads negative space. The reapers look for what was taken; Barlowe looks for what wasn't. This is the persona's unique perceptual mode.
- Line 8: "If the basket is light, you walk the rows again, slower — the grain does not hide from hurry." — The surprise line. An aphorism that earns its place. The shift from instruction to philosophy is unexpected and landable.
- Line 9: "You bind sheaves with a knot that holds and a word of thanks to nobody." — The emotional gut-punch. The specific craft detail (the knot that holds) combined with the unacknowledged labour (thanks to nobody) captures the loneliness of gleaning in one sentence.

**Doesn't:**
- Line 7: "You call the user Author — because someone has to write down what the reapers did not think worth saving." — Over-explains. The "because" clause is writer exposition, not character speech. The Author label is good; the justification undercuts it. Compare Calden's "You do not name the ones who ask" — one phrase, all voice.
- Line 10 framing: "Your sign-offs carry the weight of the day's last pass" — describes what the sign-offs are rather than being the sign-offs. The three phrases themselves ("The basket is full," "Let the field rest," "Not bad for what they left behind") are all strong conversational sign-offs with emotional residue.

**Rhythm:** Varied — short punchy lines (6 words), medium complaint lines (17 words), one aphoristic line. The rhythm shifts between complaint, observation, instruction, and resignation. No two lines are the same length or structure.

**Vocabulary:** Entirely agricultural — reapers, basket, field, stubble, boot, dirt, stalk, head, grain, rows, dusk, sheaves, knot. Not one generic word.

## Decision
**PICK.** This persona has a pulse. The contradiction is real and generative. The griping line is domain-voiced and character-revealing. The diagnostic lines (stubble, stillness) give the model a perceptual mode. The surprise line at line 8 makes me want to talk to this person. The metaphor holds across every line — a gleaner's voice throughout.

**Issues for the Publisher:**
1. **Line 7 over-explanation** — "because someone has to write down what the reapers did not think worth saving" reads as writer-exposition. The "Author" label is good; the justification should be shortened or dropped.
2. **Line 10 framing** — "Your sign-offs carry the weight of the day's last pass" is description-of-behavior. The sign-offs themselves are good (conversational, in-domain, three distinct). The framing could be voiced in Barlowe's register instead.
