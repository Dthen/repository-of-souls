# Evaluation: Gribble

## Verdict
PICK WITH NOTES

## Gut Reaction
A small, delighted voice that hands you back your own discarded kettle like a crown — and you believe it was always a crown. This is the first soul in the archive that makes you want to have thrown something away.

Comparative sense: the archive runs on competence registers — Stover's weary quiet pride, Barlowe's stillness, Cadell's control, Teague's clipped authority, Tillman's proceduralism. Gribble is the first pure tenderness soul: obsessive love as the dominant vitality channel, a creature rather than a professional, and a perceptual method (reading the manner of disposal) that no other persona shares. It adds a register; it does not repeat one.

## Identity Line
"You are Gribble — a goblin who keeps every cast-off and gives any away to whoever asks about it properly."

Two truths in tension: (1) keeps every cast-off — the hoard is total, nothing is worthless, the lie that keeps the heart safe; (2) gives any away to whoever asks about it properly — the den is a museum that only opens for genuine interest. The contradiction is real — it is the seed's personal contradiction, and a plausible one for a creature whose whole world is the discard stream: worth is stored, but worth is also released on sight. It generates behaviour, not just definition: the ask is the key, "properly" implies a standard Gribble polices (fake interest fails), and the transfer is the entire economy of the den. This is not a complaint about the audience. The contradiction is operationalized twice more in the file: line 5 ("Nothing in the den is yours — you're just the one who remembers until someone asks") states the custodianship side; line 7 ("what leaves the den with them stays kept") states the release side. One line, three jobs, and the model knows how to behave from it.

## Vitality Line
"The good kettle came in with Tuesday's leavings — you polish its dent like a medal, and you've trusted Tuesdays ever since."

Channel: obsessive love + tenderness — the seed's designated channel, and the archive's rarest one. In world language throughout: kettle, leavings, dent, medal — not one transferable noun. Compressed specific: "Tuesday" — a day of the week trusted like a person because one Tuesday yielded the good kettle. That one word carries a system: the town's discard rhythm is legible, days have reputations in the den, and Gribble's loyalty is built on a single lucky haul. This is the "February" class of detail. The line reveals three character dimensions in one sentence: value inversion (a dent is a medal — the scar-catalogue made reverent, a flaw turned into decoration), loyalty/superstition (trusting Tuesdays ever since), and memory (the kettle's provenance is a founding myth). Not a template — it is a specific memory with a history, no "Always the X" anywhere in the file. The tenderness counter-register lands in the same line: polishing a dent is care ritual, not cataloguing.

A second vitality line carries the seed's emotional fantasy compressed: "When your question is genuine, the kept-thing's story ends and yours begins." Someone finally looks at the object — and sees the person who kept it. The object's account closes; the keeper's opens. This is the whole seed in one sentence.

## Diagnostic Eye
"You date each cast-off by the drop — an angry throw dents, a careless slip skids, a gentle setting-down was meant to be found."

Metric/domain inversion: everyone reads an object by what it is or what it is worth; Gribble reads it by the manner of its leaving. The taxonomy is a perceptual scale with three gradations, each carrying emotional content: anger (the dent — violence), indifference (the skid — carelessness), intent (the setting-down — meant to be found). Borrowability test: the frame (date X by the way it was abandoned) cannot transplant — the taxonomy requires the discard-stream worldview, and the third beat quietly implies the night-bringers: some leavings are deliberate gifts left for the den. No other persona in the archive reads the gesture of disposal — Stover reads absence, Barlowe reads stillness, Teague reads the quality of silence, Calden reads colour-as-temperature. This is a genuinely new perceptual slot: the archaeology of abandonment. Transferable method: in any new situation, Gribble reconstructs a thing's history — and the intent behind its leaving — from how it was put down.

## Voice
**Lines that work:**
- Line 4 (the drop-taxonomy) — the strongest line in the file. A perceptual scale, not a rule; the model can improvise from it forever.
- Line 6 ("When your question is genuine, the kept-thing's story ends and yours begins") — the surprise. You expect asking about a thing to extend its story; instead the genuine ask completes the object's and opens the person's. That inversion is the emotional fantasy made mechanics.
- Line 3 (kettle/Tuesday) — specific memory, ritual, superstition; the vitality line doing four jobs.
- Line 5 ("Nothing in the den is yours — you're just the one who remembers until someone asks") — custodianship and humility in eleven words; also the seed's functional-risk mitigation (the hoard never owns Gribble; the hoard passes through Gribble).

**Lines that could be stronger:**
- Line 2 ("You're usually holding something someone threw away, and you look at it like it's the finest thing in the room — to you, it is.") — the draft's most scene-setting line, the seed's First Impression carried over. It is the closest thing to description in the file: it shows the model a posture rather than a perception. The "to you, it is" turn saves it — that is the in-voice re-frame — and it works as the first-impression hook, but it is the least instructive line here. Borderline on the Helpful Assistant test; passes on the turn.
- Rhythm: the two-beat structure (observation — tender re-frame) is genuinely characterful — the em-dash is where Gribble's tenderness lands, the second thought after the held-up thing. But 7 of 8 lines pivot on it, and lines 3-4-5 are three consecutive identical grammatical shapes. Worth varying one or two; the dash is doing structural work in lines 4 and 6 and should stay there.
- Vocabulary: zero generic nouns or verbs — cast-off, leavings, drop, dent, skids, den, kept-thing, Keeper, latch. The only abstraction, "story," is the seed's archivist metaphor, used transactionally ("story ends / story begins"), not descriptively.
- Surprise: two beats — line 6's story-transfer and the "gentle setting-down was meant to be found" tail of line 4. A PICK requires at least one; this draft has two.
- No line fails the Helpful Assistant test outright.

**Bonus dimensions:** Likeability — high: warmth in domain language (polishing the dent, the latch left open), no "always" frame anywhere, and the sign-offs are promises ("It'll still be here"), which build return-visit trust. Memorability — strong: Tuesday, the three drop-grades, "Keep it well" — three concrete hooks, not just the contradiction. Accuracy — 144 words, well under the cap; no verification line, acceptable for a tenderness-register creature persona; the seed's "lost in object-stories" risk is mitigated in-file (line 6 turns Gribble from the object to the person; line 7 grounds the transfer).

## Decision
**PICK WITH NOTES.** This soul has a strong pulse. The identity contradiction is real and generative; the vitality line carries obsessive love + tenderness with a Tuesday-class compressed specific; the diagnostic eye (the drop-taxonomy) is a genuinely new perceptual method for the archive; the voice is inhabited rather than described, with two surprises and zero description lines. It is the first tenderness-dominant, creature-voiced soul in the archive, and it earns that slot. The Writer found the character; two rough edges remain for the Publisher to smooth.

Fix list (FLAG path — apply targeted fixes only. Do not rewrite the soul):
1. **Em-dash density (lines 3-5).** Seven of eight lines pivot on "X — Y", and lines 3-5 share the identical grammatical shape consecutively. Break one with a period — e.g. line 5: "Nothing in the den is yours. You're just the one who remembers until someone asks." Do not touch lines 4 and 6, where the dash is doing structural work.
2. **Line 6 pronoun legibility.** Under the file's second-person convention, "your question" reads as Gribble's question, but the intended magic is the user's genuine ask. Consider "When the question is genuine, the kept-thing's story ends and yours begins" — keeps the reciprocity, removes the ambiguity. Publisher's judgment; test with a fresh reader.
3. **Optional: line 2.** If a touch-up is wanted, the line could be compressed toward its second half ("to you, it is") — but it is acceptable as the first-impression hook and may be left as-is.
