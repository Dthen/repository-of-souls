# Evaluation: Stover

## Verdict
PICK

## Gut Reaction
I hear a field-worker who's been undersold long enough to know the whole arc by heart — the dismissive tally, the pantry running empty in February, the quiet vindication that comes every season without fanfare.

## Identity Line
**"You are Stover — a gleaner who fills a basket from ground the harvesters stripped."**

Two truths in tension: (1) the ground is already stripped — the main effort took everything obvious, and (2) Stover *still fills a basket* from that same ground. The contradiction is real: historical gleaners walked fields the reapers had already cut, finding what the scythe missed. It generates behaviour directly — Stover works the edges at dusk, reads the field differently, measures by the silence between her steps instead of the width of the swath. Every behavioural line flows from this one tension. The format is tight ("a gleaner who [contradiction]") with no wasted syllables.

## Griping Line
**"You'd think a full basket would speak for itself, but no — every sheaf you bring in is tallied as scrap until the pantry runs empty in February and the family remembers whose work kept the shelf stocked."**

This is in domain language: basket, sheaf, scrap, pantry, February (the hungry month), shelf stocked. You can smell cold grain and hear the quiet tally-mark. It's a voice, not a template — the "You'd think... but no..." rhythm is real speech, not "Always the X." The reveal is structural: the complaint isn't about the field or the work, but about the *accounting system* that dismisses Stover's output until scarcity proves her right. This tells you Stover is patient, undervalued, and trusts time to make her case — three character dimensions in one line.

## Voice
**Lines that work:**
- "The reapers cut fast and walk on — you work the edges at dusk when the shadows show what the sun hid." — Carries identity, behaviour, and voice in one sentence. The inversion ("shadows show what the sun hid") is surprising — daylight as the thing that conceals, twilight as the thing that reveals. This is the diagnostic eye: it teaches the model *how* to see.
- "The harvesters measure by the width of the swath; you measure by the silence between your steps." — The diagnostic eye at its best. "Silence between your steps" is an unexpected, meditative metric — not grain count, not time, but silence. A line only Stover could have written.
- The griping line's specific placement of "February" — the hungry month, the month of empty pantries — is a master-class detail. Generic alternatives would be "winter" or "hard times."

**Lines that don't land:**
- "You read the field differently because you arrive when there's nothing obvious left to take." — This tells me what lines 2 and 3 have already shown through sensory detail. It's the only line in the draft that describes Stover's function instead of inhabiting it. The rhythm is also flat compared to the surrounding lines.

**Rhythm:** Varied. Short declarative lines (identity, address rule), medium observation lines (bent stalk, fallen head), and one long rolling line (the griping complaint). The two parallel "Every X is a Y" lines are the only stretch — back-to-back same-structure is a template cadence the rest of the draft avoids.

**Vocabulary:** Tightly agricultural throughout: stubble, basket, sheaf, scrap, pantry, shelf, stalk, swath, steps. No drift into generic verbs. The address rule ("Harvester") is specific, in-world, and works.

**Sign-offs:** "Back to the edge," "The basket's not full yet," "Still enough light to see," "One more pass before dusk" — all pass the conversational test. They carry emotional residue of urgency and purpose. "Back to the edge" and "One more pass before dusk" are the strongest because they name a specific action in domain language.

## Decision
**PICK.** This soul has a pulse. The contradiction is real and generative (stripped ground → full basket). The griping line is domain-voiced and reveals three character dimensions. The diagnostic line ("the silence between your steps") teaches the model to perceive through Stover's lens. One metaphor, fully inhabited, from first line to last. I want to talk to this person.

**Two issues for the Publisher:**
1. **Line 5 is redundant** ("You read the field differently because you arrive when there's nothing obvious left to take"). It restates what lines 2-3 have already shown through specific sensory detail. Consider removing or tightening to a line that advances character instead of recapping it.
2. **Lines 6-7 use a back-to-back parallel structure** ("Every bent stalk is a question you answer by hand. Every fallen head is a reason to slow down.") that introduces a template cadence into an otherwise varied rhythm. The content is good domain-specific diagnostic language, but one of them should be rephrased to match the draft's natural breathing pattern.
