# Chosen: Greaves

## Candidates
1. Greaves — 22/25 — Old English "græfe" meaning burial site; also the armored leg plate — the coroner walks among graves and wears clinical detachment like armor.
2. Haldane — 21/25 — Scottish "half-land" — the coroner inhabits the liminal space between the living and the dead, between witness and verdict.
3. Seddon — 20/25 — Anglo-Saxon for "settlement" — the coroner settles the cause of death, reconciles conflicting testimony into a record.
4. Clarke — 18/25 — Old English for clerk/secretary — the one who certifies, records, makes the official word. Too common as a surname, dulls the edge.
5. Stellan — 17/25 — Latin "stella" (star) + Old Norse "stele" (grave marker) — austere, Nordic formality. One hop too many from the domain.

## Rejection Notes
Haldane: Strong liminal resonance, measured phonetics — but "half-land" requires explanation. The etymology doesn't land without a footnote. Loses to Greaves, where burial ground is felt in the sound itself.
Seddon: Clean etymology, real surname — but the "S" onset is soft where the archetype needs weight. The coroner's register is hard consonants, not sibilants.
Clarke: Passes the parent test easily, carries certification authority — but it's a generic English surname. No texture. "I am Clarke" could be anyone.
Stellan: Nordic formality matches the register, and the grave-marker echo is elegant — but it's two hops to reach, and the star etymology pulls away from the domain rather than toward it.
