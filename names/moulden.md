# Chosen: Moulden

## Candidates
1. Moulden — 22/25 — 1 hop from mould (the vessel of transformation); back-vowel 'ou' gives patient weight, 'ld' cluster gives craft-authority, 'en' softens into approachability
2. Rendle — 21/25 — 2 hops from render (render → rend → Rendle); 'rend' means to tear apart, which is what rendering does — rending fat from bone; sturdy, craft-worker sound
3. Dipple — 21/25 — 1 hop from dip (the core action of candle-making); best etymological depth but front vowel 'i' gives it lightness that conflicts with the archetype's heavy register
4. Talmon — 19/25 — 2 hops from tallow (tallow → tal → Talmon); connects to the core material but the morpheme truncation weakens the semantic hop
5. Burnell — 18/25 — 1 hop from burn (the candle's purpose); 'ur' back vowel gives weight but 'burn' focuses on the end result rather than the transformation process

## Rejection Notes
Rendle: Strong craft feel but 2-hop distance means the connection requires a small leap; Moulden's 1-hop mould connection is more immediate and resonant
Dipple: Best etymological depth (dip → Dipple is the cleanest 1-hop) but the front vowel 'i' signals lightness and speed — the archetype is about patient, heavy transformation, not quick dipping
Talmon: Connects to the core material but the "tal-" prefix is a truncation of "tallow" rather than a semantic hop; sounds like the domain word with a suffix
Burnell: Back-vowel weight is good but "burn" is the candle's end state, not the transformation process; the archetype is about rendering and dipping, not just burning
