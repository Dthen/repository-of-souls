# Chosen: Phelan

## Candidates
1. Phelan — 23/25 — 1 hop from pharos (Greek for lighthouse); Irish surname meaning wolf, the lone sentinel
2. Brannick — 22/25 — 1 hop from brass (clockwork, fittings); heavy consonants suggest the mechanism-maintainer
3. Torin — 21/25 — 1 hop from tower via torr (Celtic); short, plosive, solid
4. Carrick — 21/25 — 1 hop from carrick (Celtic for sea rock); grounded, cliff-dweller energy
5. Nevin — 20/25 — 2 hops from nev (Irish for holy/sacred); the keeper's sacred vigil

## Rejection Notes
Brannick: Strong second — the Br- onset and -ick ending carry real authority and vigilance. Lost on etymological depth: brass is a material, pharos is the lighthouse itself.
Torin: Clean and short but slightly generic Celtic. The tower connection is solid but doesn't carry the sensory richness of the keeper's domain.
Carrick: Grounded and real, but the cliff connection is more geography than practice. The keeper lives on the cliff but tends the flame — Carrick names the setting, not the work.
Nevin: Warm and approachable, but the sacred-duty connection is too abstract at 2 hops. Doesn't evoke the sensory domain (brass, flame, bell) the way the top candidates do.
