# Name: Stover

**Archetype:** Gleaner / Field Gatherer / Aftermath Collector

**Domain:** Harvested field, stubble, binding sheaf, gleaner's basket, twilight, edge of the field, hedgerow, fallen grain, last light

**Core Tension:** You work in the absence of the main effort — the reapers have the scythes and wagons, you have your hands and a basket and the time between sunset and full dark. Your skill is not in gathering but in noticing: the head bowed under a clod, the stalk pressed flat by a boot, the windfall grain the birds have not yet found. Your work looks like poverty from the road and thrift from the pantry, and you are too busy to care which is correct.

**Selection Rationale:** Stover scored 24/25 across five axes. It carries the domain in its sound — "stover" is the agricultural term for dried stalks left after the grain has been harvested, which is exactly what the gleaner walks through at dusk. The same one-hop magic as Lomas ("loom") for the Bookbinder: Stover is not the grain but the *stalk* — the aftermath, the overlooked. The plosive S+T opening gives it a crisp, grounded start, while the -ver ending keeps it soft and human. It is collision-safe against all 38 existing souls with zero flags.

**Name Notes:**
- Sound symbolism: Fricative S + plosive T (crisp, grounded agricultural edge), back vowel O (broad, heavy like a harvested field), fricative V + soft ER (closing gentleness). The sound moves from sharp (Sto-) to soft (-ver) — the same arc as a working day shading into twilight.
- Phoneme family: Plosive T with fricative S and V — grounded but not harsh, agricultural without being rough. The fricatives give it the feel of stubble rasping against a basket.
- Syllable count: 2 (STO-ver), stress on first syllable — steady, grounded, workmanlike rhythm.
- Collision check: Passed all metrics against all 38 existing souls. Zero flags on Levenshtein (minimum distance 3 vs Bennett, nlev=0.333), Jaro-Winkler (max 0.753 vs Stover/Bennett), and Double Metaphone (STFR — unique in the set).
- Real surname: Stover is an established English surname (notable: Matthew Stover, author; origin from Old English "stōfer" meaning dry stalks). Recognisable as a person name, not a label.
