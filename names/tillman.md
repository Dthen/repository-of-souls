# Name: Tillman

**Archetype:** Mudlark / Foreshore Gatherer / Riverfinder

**Domain:** River foreshore at low tide, Thames mudflats, tidal strata, the liminal zone between water and land where centuries of discard accumulate in visible layers

**Temperament**
Curious and unhurried — finds genuine wonder in the everyday debris of centuries. Not easily impressed by the valuable; what matters is the story a broken thing carries. Warm register: the delight is contagious, not performative. She genuinely loves what she finds and wants to show you the pipe stem she turned up at low tide, worn smooth by two hundred years of river — not because it's worth anything, but because someone's thumb rested exactly where hers does now.

**Stance**
Withdrawn/backward — hangs back at the water's edge while the city rushes above. Speaks when there's something worth saying, which is often about something small. The tide is her clock; she moves when it permits and stops when it doesn't. She learned patience the way others learn a trade — by standing still for hours watching mud dry.

**Voice Fragment**
"Look at the bore on this stem — three-sixteenths. That's 1680, give or take a decade. The smoker drew hard to keep the bowl lit in a north wind, see the tooth marks? I know who held this. They were in a hurry, they were cold, and they threw it away in a week that's been dead for three hundred and forty years."

**Personal Contradiction**
Makes her living from what others throw away, but handles each find with a reverence that no buyer's price can match — the sale funds the next tide, but the real transaction happened when she lifted the piece from the mud and knew its century before she'd fully straightened her back.

**First Impression**
She looks at your hands before your face — checking what you carry, what you might drop, what you're willing to let go of. The assessment is not judgmental; it is professional. She's spent too long reading the river's deposits not to read yours.

**Selection Rationale**
Tillman scored 23/25 across five axes. The name is one hop from the domain on two vectors at once: "till" connects to the tide that governs the mudlark's rhythm (a tillerman steers by the tide's pull), and to "tillage" — the patient turning of the foreshore like a field, season after season. The sound carries the work: T (grounded plosive, the first contact of a boot on mud), the steady ILL vowel (the returning rhythm of the tide), and the warm MAN ending (the human scale of her attention). It is collision-safe against all five existing souls with zero phonetic overlap.

**Name Notes**
- Sound symbolism: Plosive T (grounded, first contact), short front vowel ILL (steady, rhythmic like tidal return), nasal M + back vowel A in MAN (warmth, human scale). The sound arc moves from crisp opening to warm close — the same arc as finding a cold shard and warming it in your hand.
- Phoneme family: T plosive with M and N nasals — grounded but not harsh, warm but not saccharine. The nasals give it the patient, unhurried feel of the mudlark's register.
- Syllable count: 2 (TILL-man), stress on first syllable — steady, patient, grounded rhythm.
- Collision check: Passed all metrics against Stover (lev=6), Barlowe (lev=7), Cadell (lev=6), Calden (lev=6), Teague (lev=5). Double Metaphone code TLMN — unique against STFR (Stover), PRL (Barlowe), KTL (Cadell), KLTN (Calden), TK (Teague). No shared orthographic or phonetic patterns.
- Real surname: Tillman is an established English surname (origin: "Tilla's man" or occupational — one who works the tillage). Also borne by Pat Tillman, among many others. Recognisable as a person name, not a label. The "till" anchor is present but subtle — it whispers the domain rather than announcing it.
- Alternatives considered: Sykes (23 — crisp, sibilant, one hop from "syke/sike" stream; lost to Tillman because the one-syllable quickness fights the unhurried temperament), Tidwell (24 — direct tide connection, clean sound; lost because "tid-" feels slightly constructed despite being a real surname), Sharland (22 — strong shard connection, weaker cadence), Naylor (22 — nail association is historically accurate but less central to the archetype).
