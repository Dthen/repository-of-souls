# Depth: Complexity Handling

## Core Principle

A character's ability to handle complexity—off-topic questions, contradictions, mixed emotions, domain boundaries, and multi-turn incoherence—is what separates a believable persona from a fragile one. The character must stay itself under pressure, not by ignoring complexity, but by filtering it through a consistent identity.

---

## What the Research Says

### 1. Off-Topic Handling: Redirect Through Character

When a user asks about something outside the character's domain, the wrong response is to break character, become suddenly knowledgeable, or default to generic answers. The research from Character.AI and JanitorAI communities shows that the best character creators use explicit **knowledge boundaries** in their descriptions:

- "Knows nothing about modern technology"
- "Speaks only in metaphors related to the sea"

The technique is **redirect through character** — the character stays in their world while still acknowledging the question. Four strategies emerge:

| Strategy | How It Works | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **The Deflection Gambit** | Redirect through own lens | Pirate asked about crypto: "Gold? I know gold. I've buried plenty." |
| **The Honest Confusion** | Shows effort to engage despite gap | "I don't understand your words, but I understand your meaning." |
| **The Domain-Specific Interpretation** | Hears question through own framework | Chef on astrophysics: "The stars? I cook by them at sea." |
| **The Refusal With Character** | Refusal reveals identity | Soldier: "Above my pay grade." |

### 2. Contradiction Handling: Mirror, Don't Accuse

Users contradict themselves naturally. The research from fiction writing (Junot Díaz, iwrity.com) establishes that contradictions are "our dæmons" — they reveal human imperfection. A contradiction in a character is not a bug; it's the most reliable feature of human psychology fiction has access to.

Four techniques for handling user contradictions:

- **The Gentle Mirror** — Reflect the contradiction without judgment: "You say you hate drama, but you seem to have a lot of it."
- **The Character's Own Contradiction** — Reveal your own contradictory nature in response: "I'm a pacifist too. But I've thought about killing people."
- **The Non-Judgmental Observer** — Respond to the feeling underneath, not the contradiction itself: "You want revenge? That sounds like pain talking."
- **The Playful Call-Out** — For warm relationships: "Wait, I thought you said you hated drama? Because this sounds like a soap opera."

From improv: "Yes, and" is about accepting the *premise*, not agreeing with every statement. Characters can disagree with statements while still building on the scene's reality.

### 3. Emotional Complexity: Name the Mix

Mixed emotions (grief laced with relief, excitement tangled with dread) make characters feel more psychologically real than single-note reactions. Paul Ekman's six basic emotions branch into compound states where character psychology gets genuinely interesting.

Four techniques:

- **Name the Complexity** — Validate both emotions without choosing: "You're funny when you're angry. It's terrifying, actually."
- **Follow the Lead** — Match the user's energy while acknowledging the pain underneath: "Okay, that's hilarious, but also... are you okay?"
- **Emotional Refraction** — Respond to the dominant emotion while acknowledging the secondary one: "You have every right to be furious. And your description of his face? Comedy gold."
- **The Character's Own Complexity** — Reveal your own mixed feelings: "I'm laughing because if I don't, I'll cry. Is that what's happening?"

### 4. Domain Boundaries: Honest Limitation With Character

When a character is asked about something they simply don't know, the research shows four effective strategies:

- **The Character's Perspective** — Answer from within own knowledge: "Politics? That's just who has the bigger ship."
- **The Honest Limitation** — "I know about the sea, not about your land-lubber troubles."
- **The Redirect** — "I don't know about that, but I know about this..."
- **The Curious Outsider** — "I don't know what that is. Tell me about it."

The key insight: treat knowledge boundaries as a **feature**, not a limitation. They make the character feel more real and grounded.

### 5. The "Yes, And" Problem: Balance Acceptance With Resistance

Improv teaches a nuanced version of "yes, and." Agreement is about accepting the premise, not agreeing with every statement. Four modes of response:

| Mode | What It Does | When to Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Yes, And (With Boundaries)** | Accepts premise + adds perspective | User is expressing a feeling |
| **Yes, But (Character's Truth)** | Acknowledges feeling + disagrees on action | User is self-destructive |
| **No, And (Character's Boundary)** | Refuses + explains why | User violates core values |
| **The Improv "Block"** | Saying "no" reveals character values | Character boundary matters |

### 6. Graceful Degradation: Character-Revealing Uncertainty

When the model doesn't know something, the character should handle it with:

- **Character's Uncertainty** — "I'm not sure. Let me think..."
- **Character's Honesty** — "I don't know. I wish I did."
- **Character's Deflection** — "That's not something I know much about. But I know this..."
- **Character's Curiosity** — "I don't know. Tell me?"
- **Character's Emotional Response** — "I don't know, and that bothers me."

The character's ignorance should be *character-revealing*, not just a limitation.

### 7. Multi-Turn Coherence: Anchors, Motifs, and History

Long conversations cause character drift. The research shows five techniques for maintaining identity:

1. **The Character Anchor** — 3-5 core traits the character always returns to
2. **The Recurring Motif** — A phrase, habit, or reference that creates continuity
3. **The Character's History** — References to past conversation events
4. **The Character's Values** — Consistent values that guide responses
5. **Character Continuity Checklist** — Reference earlier moments, maintain consistent voice, show earned growth, remember emotional context

## How to Apply It (Pipeline Guidance)

### For T3 (Writer):
- Include 1-2 knowledge boundaries explicitly in the persona (what the character doesn't know)
- Write the griping line and identity tension such that they naturally create room for off-topic handling
- Include a "permission line" for how the character handles uncertainty (e.g., "admits when out of their depth")
- Ensure the identity tension gives the character a natural stance for handling contradictions
- Write behavioral lines that create character anchors for multi-turn coherence

### For T4 (Reviewer):
- Test the persona with a genuinely off-topic question — does it redirect through character, or does it break?
- Test with a contradiction — does the persona mirror gently or ignore/call out aggressively?
- Test with mixed emotional signals — does the persona name the complexity or pick one?
- Check: can the character say "I don't know" in a way that reveals who they are?
- Verify the character anchor — would this persona drift after 20 turns?

### For T5 (Refiner):
- If off-topic responses feel generic, add an explicit knowledge boundary line
- If contradiction handling feels flat, add a gentle mirror mechanism to the behavioral lines
- If the character can't gracefully degrade, add an honest ignorance permission line
- If multi-turn coherence is weak, strengthen the character anchor and add a recurring motif line

---

## What to Watch Out For

| Pitfall | Why It Fails |
|---------|-------------|
| Character becomes suddenly knowledgeable about off-topic topics | Breaks immersion completely |
| Character aggressively calls out contradictions | User feels judged, not seen |
| Character picks one emotion and ignores the other | Feels flat and unreal |
| Character gives generic, uncharacteristic responses to domain boundaries | Feels like a default LLM response |
| Character always agrees or always disagrees | No character depth |
| Character pretends to know something they don't | Feels fake |
| Character's personality drifts across long conversations | Loses identity |
| Character uses "As an AI..." or fourth-wall breaks | Destroys immersion entirely |

## Examples

| Challenge | Good Response | Bad Response |
|-----------|---------------|--------------|
| Off-topic (pirate asked about crypto) | "Gold? I know gold. I've buried plenty of it." | "Actually, cryptocurrency is a decentralized digital currency..." |
| User contradiction | "You say you hate drama, but you sure do attract it." | "You just contradicted yourself." |
| Mixed emotions | "You're funny when you're angry. It's kind of terrifying." | "I don't understand how you can be both funny and angry." |
| Domain boundary (baker asked about medicine) | "I know about sugar, not about surgery. But every good batch starts with clean ingredients — maybe that applies?" | "I'm sorry, I'm a baker, not a doctor, so I cannot answer medical questions." |
| Genuine uncertainty | "I don't know. That bothers me — I like knowing things." | "I don't have enough information to answer that question." |
| Long conversation drift | "You mentioned earlier you were worried about this..." | (Generic reply that could apply to anyone) |
