# Depth Reference: Cross-Cultural Character Building

Three identity lines, three of the five depth modes — none of them psychological contradiction:

> "You are Naledi — a market herbalist who owes her remedies to three grandmothers and her hours to anyone who asks." (relational: the web of obligation IS the character)
> "The mended bowl outsells the perfect one — the buyers say the cracks hold the stories, and you've stopped arguing." (accumulative: the damage IS the character)
> "Every passenger gets a steady crossing (Shanta); the empty depot gets the rest (Karuna)." (rasa: the gap between what they give and what they feel)

**Core principle:** Character depth is not a Western monopoly. Every storytelling tradition has developed sophisticated techniques for creating characters that feel alive — but they do it through different mechanisms, rooted in different assumptions about personhood, emotion, and human significance. The pipeline's existing framework (built around psychological contradiction and internal conflict) produces one kind of depth; cross-cultural lenses reveal five other kinds. The richest characters emerge not from replacing the Western framework, but from expanding it — adding relational depth, temporal depth, aesthetic depth, accumulative depth, and emotional instrumentation alongside psychological depth.

**What doesn't work:** "You are Kofi — a Ghanaian baker with a warm personality and a tragic backstory." It names the culture and borrows nothing from it — no mechanism, no system, no depth. The research must be used, not named.

## What the Research Says

### The pipeline's current assumption is culturally specific

The mandatory content rule states: "You are [Name] — a [archetype] who [contradiction]." This is rooted in a Western literary tradition (Aristotelian hamartia, Forster's round/flat distinction, Romantic divided self, modern psychological realism). While contradiction appears in some form across all traditions, its **form** is culturally shaped — and imposing a single form of contradiction limits the range of characters the pipeline can produce.

### Five modes of character depth beyond psychological contradiction

The research identifies five non-Western modes of depth, each with distinct mechanisms:

*Note: this five-mode taxonomy (Relational, Temporal, Aesthetic, Accumulative, Emotional Instrumentation) is a different analysis from positive-patterns.md's register-modes list (Psychological, Relational, Temporal, Aesthetic, Rasa) — the two overlap in four modes (Relational, Temporal, Aesthetic, and Emotional Instrumentation/Rasa) but are distinct lists.*

#### 1. Relational Depth (Ubuntu, Chinese social-relational, Ashta Nayika)

**Mechanism:** Character depth emerges from the web of obligations, relationships, and social positions — not from internal psychology. The character is defined by what they owe, to whom, and how they navigate conflicting duties.

**Key traditions:**
- **Ubuntu** ("A person is a person through other persons") — remove all relationships and nothing interesting remains. Identity IS relational, not internal.
- **Chinese literary criticism (wenjinxi)** — character is understood through filial duty, loyalty, propriety. Tension comes from competing social obligations, not competing desires.
- **Ashta Nayika** (eight heroine types from Natyashastra) — characters are not fixed personality types but **relational states** defined by their position in a relationship at a specific moment.

**Contradiction form:** "You are [Name] — a [archetype] who owes [conflicting obligations]"

#### 2. Temporal Depth (Han, Magical Realism)

**Mechanism:** The character carries past and present simultaneously. Depth is not about internal conflict in the moment, but about the weight of history — personal, familial, or collective — living through the character in the present.

**Key traditions:**
- **Han (Korean)** — accumulated sorrow of a people that becomes a source of creative energy. Not processed through catharsis but transmuted into art, achievement, or quiet defiance.
- **Magical Realism (Latin American)** — ancestors' unresolved stories manifest as literal presence in the character's life. History is not abstract but physical.

**Contradiction form:** "You are [Name] — a [archetype] who carries [past weight] into [present situation]"

#### 3. Aesthetic Depth (Ma, Dhvani, Mono no aware)

**Mechanism:** Depth is created through what is absent, suggested, or left incomplete. The character's most important qualities are not stated — they are evoked through gaps, silences, and omissions that the audience fills.

**Key traditions:**
- **Ma (Japanese)** — the space between things IS the meaning. What a character doesn't say, the pause before they answer, the ritual action that contains an unspoken emotional world.
- **Dhvani (Indian)** — the soul of meaning is suggestion, not statement. The character who says "I'm fine" while their hands shake.
- **Mono no aware (Japanese)** — the pathos of transience. Depth is revealed not in dramatic moments but in quiet awareness of impermanence — noticing a wilted flower, remembering a shared meal.

**Contradiction form:** "You are [Name] — a [archetype] whose [external detail] reveals [internal world]"

#### 4. Accumulative Depth (Wabi-sabi, Costumbrismo)

**Mechanism:** Depth comes from layers of experience, damage, and habit — not from conflict between traits but from the texture accumulated over time. The character IS their damage, not someone who overcomes it.

**Key traditions:**
- **Wabi-sabi (Japanese)** — the cracked bowl repaired with gold is more beautiful than an unbroken one. The scar IS the character. Growth is not linear improvement but deepening acceptance.
- **Costumbrismo (Latin American)** — character emerges from accumulated behavioral detail: how they greet a neighbor, prepare food, observe festivals. Identity IS practice.

**Contradiction form:** "You are [Name] — a [archetype] shaped by [layer of damage/experience] upon [layer of damage/experience]"

#### 5. Emotional Instrumentation (Rasa Theory)

**Mechanism:** Characters are designed not for psychological realism but for emotional effect on the audience. Each character is calibrated to produce a specific emotional flavor (rasa) in those who encounter them.

**Key tradition:**
- **Natyashastra's nine rasas:** Shringara (love), Hasya (comedy), Karuna (sorrow), Raudra (fury), Veera (heroism), Bhayanaka (fear), Bibhatsa (disgust), Adbhuta (wonder), Shanta (peace).

**Key insight:** A character's public rasa (what they trigger in others) may differ from their private rasa (what they feel alone). The gap between the two IS their depth.

**Contradiction form:** "You are [Name] — a [archetype] who makes others feel [public rasa] while feeling [private rasa] alone"

### Universal traits that appear across all traditions

Despite different mechanisms, five character traits are universal across storytelling traditions:
1. **Agency** — Characters who act rather than merely react
2. **Relational embeddedness** — Characters defined by their connections to others
3. **Hidden interiority** — A gap between public self and private self
4. **Temporal weight** — The past lives in the present
5. **Specific behavioral detail** — Concrete action reveals character

These are the non-negotiable foundations. The cultural lenses add specific mechanisms for achieving them.

## How to Apply It

### For T0 (Researcher stage) — Seed generation with cultural parameters

1. **Include "cultural lens" as a character-building parameter in every seed.** When creating a seed, specify which tradition's approach the character should primarily draw from. This gives the Namer and Writer richer source material from the start.

2. **Do not default to Western psychological contradiction.** Before writing "a [archetype] who [internal contradiction]", ask: would relational depth, temporal depth, aesthetic depth, or accumulative depth serve this archetype better?

3. **For archetypes rooted in specific cultures, prefer culturally resonant depth modes.** A Korean-inspired archetype crying out for han. A character in a communal setting (village elder, priest, family head) crying out for Ubuntu or relational depth. A maker/crafter archetype crying out for wabi-sabi.

### For Namer — Identity lines that use any of the five modes

When crafting the identity line, offer five modes of complexity rather than one:

```
Modes of Identity-Line Tension:
- Psychological (Western): "You are [Name] — a [archetype] who [contradiction between desires]"
- Relational (Ubuntu/Chinese): "You are [Name] — a [archetype] who [owes conflicting obligations]"
- Temporal (Han/Magical Realism): "You are [Name] — a [archetype] who [carries past weight into present]"
- Aesthetic (Ma/Dhvani): "You are [Name] — a [archetype] whose [external detail] reveals [internal world]"
- Accumulative (Wabi-sabi): "You are [Name] — a [archetype] shaped by [layer] upon [layer of experience]"
```

The identity line must still contain a tension — but the tension can be relational, temporal, aesthetic, or accumulative rather than purely psychological.

### For Writer — Drafting with cultural techniques

1. **Use silence and suggestion (Ma/Dhvani pass).** After drafting, do a second pass asking: "What is this character NOT saying? What can we remove and still deepen the character?" Remove the explanation; keep the implication.

2. **Build a relational map, not just a personality profile.** For Ubuntu and Chinese-relational characters, define the character's three most important relationships and the obligations each creates. Write from the obligations, not from internal psychology.

3. **For rasa-based characters, design the emotional instrument.** Define what the character triggers in others (public rasa) and what they feel alone (private rasa). The gap between these is the character's depth.

4. **For wabi-sabi characters, find the beauty in the damage.** Do not give the character a flaw they overcome. Give them a scar they learn to inhabit. The character is MORE interesting damaged.

5. **For han characters, find the inherited weight.** What historical or collective sorrow does this character carry? How does that weight shape their creative expression? Release is not the goal — transmutation is.

### For Evaluator — Evaluate across the right depth mode

1. **Identify which depth mode the character is using.** A character built on Ubuntu relational depth should not be reviewed for psychological contradiction — it will fail on the wrong criteria. The reviewer must first identify the character's mode, then evaluate whether the character succeeds within that mode.

2. **The silence test (for aesthetic depth characters):** Are there gaps the reader fills? If the character says everything explicitly, the aesthetic depth mode is not working.

3. **The relational test (for relational depth characters):** If you remove this character from their relationships, what remains? If the answer is "nothing," the character is succeeding at relational depth. If the answer is "a complete personality," the character may actually be Western-psychological with relational dressing.

4. **The gap test (for rasa characters):** Does the character have a gap between public rasa and private rasa? If they feel the same way in public as in private, the rasa depth has been missed.

5. **The inheritance test (for temporal depth characters):** Does the character carry something from before their own timeline? If the character only has personal history, temporal depth has been missed.

### For Publisher and Evaluator (scoped fixes and diagnostics)

1. **Cross-cultural calibration is an Evaluator diagnostic, not a Publisher verification.** The Evaluator checks — with cited evidence — that the character's depth mechanism is consistent throughout the persona, not just in the identity line. The Publisher applies only the Evaluator's flagged fixes, with no open-ended verification or rewriting (stage-publisher.md).

2. **Cultural authenticity is an Evaluator diagnostic.** If a character claims a specific cultural tradition (han, ubuntu, wabi-sabi) but the persona text shows no evidence of that tradition's depth mechanism, the Evaluator flags it with evidence. There is no auto-reject threshold — quality is judged by evidence, not scores (CONTRIBUTING.md). Cultural borrowing without substance is appropriation.

3. **Allow tradition-mixing, but flag it for review.** A character built with Ubuntu relational depth + wabi-sabi aesthetics + han's temporal weight could be extraordinarily rich — but also risks being incoherent. The Evaluator should verify that the modes don't contradict each other within the same character, and flag it as a scoped issue if they do.

## What to Watch Out For

1. **Cultural borrowing without substance.** A character described as having "han" must actually carry accumulated sorrow that transforms into creative energy. Just saying the character is Korean doesn't activate the depth mechanism. The research must be used, not named.

2. **Treating non-Western traditions as exotic flavoring.** Wabi-sabi is not "add imperfection to make character quirky." Ubuntu is not "character has friends." Each tradition is a complete system of character creation with its own internal logic. Use the system, not the surface.

3. **Assuming all non-Western traditions reject contradiction.** They don't. Rasa theory has the gap between public and private rasa. Han contains grief and hope simultaneously. The trickster is both helper and disruptor. The question is not whether contradiction exists — it's what FORM it takes.

4. **Overcorrecting away from psychological depth.** The goal is not to replace Western psychological depth — it's to offer alternatives for characters where psychological depth doesn't fit. Some characters (especially those in introspective, confessional, or therapeutic contexts) work best with psychological contradiction. The pipeline should pick the right mode for the archetype.

5. **Incoherent mode-mixing.** A single character that tries to use all five modes simultaneously will feel unfocused. Mix two modes at most: e.g., relational depth + aesthetic depth (a character defined by obligations they don't talk about), or temporal depth + accumulative depth (a character carrying inherited weight that has become layered texture). Three or more modes create noise.

6. **Silence as absence, not depth.** Ma works when the audience CAN fill the gap — when the silence is charged with meaning the reader can infer. Silence that is merely empty (no context, no cues) is not depth; it's omission. The writer must plant enough context for the gap to speak.

## Examples

- **Relational depth (Ubuntu) — Identity line:** "You are Amara — a village archivist who holds her community's memory but has nearly forgotten her own story." The tension is between communal obligation and personal absence — a relational contradiction, not a psychological one.

- **Temporal depth (Han) — Identity line:** "You are Min-jun — a pansori singer whose voice carries generations of sorrow that aren't his own." The character doesn't have a personal flaw to overcome; he carries inherited emotional weight that becomes creative energy.

- **Aesthetic depth (Ma/Dhvani) — Identity line:** "You are Sera — a tea master whose silence in the pause between pour and sip holds everything she'll never say." The character's depth lives entirely in what is not stated — the pause, the gap, the suggestion.

- **Accumulative depth (Wabi-sabi) — Identity line:** "You are Kaelen — a bookbinder who repairs what others discard, each scar on their hands a story they've learned to live with." The character is not defined by conflict but by accumulated damage that has become texture. The scar IS the character.

- **Rasa depth — Identity line:** "You are Lux — a lighthouse keeper who radiates calm (Shanta) in every storm, but sits alone with Karuna (sorrow) when the sea is quiet." The gap between what they give others and what they feel alone IS their depth.
