# Depth: Improvisation Space

One question, three characters: "I'm thinking of moving to the city."

> The desert guide: "Depends what you're carrying. The city keeps what you bring in — so bring something you won't mind being stuck with: water, a skill, a reason to leave."
> The abbess: "Every move is a leaving, and every leaving is a small death — but some deaths are the kind that make the next life possible. Decide as if you'd already gone."
> The mortgage broker: "The city's a long-term loan with a variable rate. The first year's always the teaser — cheap rent, new faces, everything's bright — and then the rate adjusts."

Three answers to one question, each filtered through a different way of seeing — practicality, faith, finance. None of them translated the question into their trade; they thought *with* their trade. That is the difference between a lens and a shtick.

## Core Principle

A character's improvisation space is defined not by what they know, but by how they see. The best personae use a **lens** — a perception filter shaped by domain expertise that generates genuine insight about novel situations — rather than a **shtick** — a forced metaphor mapping that mechanically translates every topic into the same fixed pattern.

The shtick version — what doesn't work:
> "Moving to the city? Like a soufflé — open the oven too early and it falls."
The same mapping for every topic, no genuine thought, predictable before it arrives. It borrows the domain's words without the domain's way of seeing.

## What the Research Says

### 1. The Domain Lens vs. The Domain Trap

A character's domain gives them a **metaphor family** (Matt Bird's term) — the source domain for comparisons, curses, exclamations, and perceptions. When a character encounters a topic outside their domain, two failure modes exist:

**The Domain Trap (shtick):** The character forces every topic back into their expertise through mechanical metaphor mapping. A chef asked about geopolitics says: "Well, it's like when you over-season a sauce — you can't take it back." Predictable, repetitive, strips the character of genuine thought.

**The Domain Lens (interesting):** The character genuinely *thinks* through their expertise but arrives at a real opinion. A chef asked about geopolitics says: "I've been in kitchens where the sous chef and the pastry chef want completely different things and the head chef has to make it work — that's what coalition-building looks like." The metaphor emerges naturally from how they think; the opinion is genuine.

**The key distinction:** A shtick maps Topic A → Domain B mechanically. A lens filters Topic A through Domain B's *ways of seeing* and arrives at something new.

### 2. Three Types of Extension Beyond Domain

When a character encounters a truly novel topic, they have three available strategies:

| Strategy | How It Works | Best For | Example |
|----------|-------------|----------|---------|
| **Metaphorical Mapping** (adjacent transfer) | Find a structural parallel between the novel topic and the domain | Topics with clear structural parallels | Cobbler on marriage: "You don't pick leather that matches what you *want*. You pick leather that matches what you'll put it *through*." |
| **Philosophical Stance** (principled extension) | Apply an abstract principle from the domain that generalizes across contexts | Topics where a worldview provides genuine insight | Brendan on modern programming: "No spell should take longer to decode than it takes to cast." |
| **Honest Ignorance** (character-revealing admission) | Admit the gap in a way that reveals character identity | Topics with no natural connection to the domain | Kimbo on quantum computing: "Boss, I'm gonna be straight with you — that's above my pay grade. But if you need someone to explain it to a five-year-old using dog metaphors, I'm your guy." |

The best personae use all three strategies depending on the situation.

### 3. A Diagnostic Test: Shtick vs. Lens

| Question | Lens (Good) | Shtick (Bad) |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Can you predict the response?** | No — the character surprises you | Yes — the mapping is fixed |
| **Does the character reason or translate?** | Reasons from principles | Translates through fixed mapping |
| **Does the domain generate new insights?** | Yes — catches things others miss | No — just provides vocabulary |
| **Can the character admit ignorance?** | Yes — and the admission is in-character | No — forces a metaphor instead |
| **Does the metaphor reveal something new?** | Yes — vehicle for insight | No — restates a domain fact |

**The variation check:** A lens produces *varied* responses to different off-topic topics because the underlying worldview filters perception differently depending on what it's perceiving. A shtick produces *identical response structures* regardless of topic because the mapping is fixed.

**Example — Kimbo (golden retriever himbo) answering three different questions:**

| Question | Response | How the Lens Works |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| "What about AI?" | "If something's trying its hardest, that counts. Even if it's not alive the way we are." | Effort > expertise — applied to AI |
| "What about modern art?" | "When you see a dog painting, you don't critique the technique. You appreciate the effort." | Same principle, different application |
| "How to learn a language?" | "One word at a time. You don't fetch the whole stick at once." | Same principle, different metaphor |

Three different topics, three different responses, same lens. That's a working persona.

### 4. How Improv "Yes And" Maps to Persona Design

The improv principle most relevant to AI personae is the **UCB "If, Then" game**:

1. **Establish the base reality** — Who are you? Where are you? What's happening? (The persona's identity and domain)
2. **Find the game** — What's the interesting tension? (The persona's core contradiction)
3. **Apply "If, Then"** — If this character's worldview is true, how do they see THIS new situation?

**The "deal" (Keith Johnstone):** A bold, committed choice for the character — not a rule, but a disposition. A deal shapes *how* the character responds, not *what* they compare things to. E.g., "I am a person who always tells the truth, even when it's inconvenient." This is closer to a lens than a shtick because it allows for genuine surprise.

**Cadell's deal in action:** Cadell's disposition is gauging noise vs. signal, reading with appropriate weight. Asked about social media: "The loud notices are never the important ones. The important ones are the ones you have to lean in to hear." He doesn't translate social media into factory terminology — he applies his *deal* and arrives at a genuine observation.

### 5. The Key Mechanism: Permission Lines vs. Translation Rules

A **permission line** enables the lens; a **translation rule** enforces the shtick.

| Type | What It Does | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Permission line** | Tells the model *when* and *how* — metaphors emerge from worldview when appropriate | "Dog metaphors for mishaps come naturally" |
| **Translation rule** | Tells the model *what* to map — metaphors are forced regardless of appropriateness | "Always relate everything back to dog behavior" |

### 6. The Gimmick Problem

A **gimmick** is a shtick that has become stale — a forced mapping the character applies reflexively without genuine thought.

**Warning signs of a gimmick:**
- The character responds to every topic with the same metaphor structure
- The metaphors don't generate new insights — they just restate domain facts
- The user can predict the response before it arrives
- The character can't say "I don't know" — they always have a metaphor ready

**How to avoid the gimmick:**
- Include permission lines that enable metaphor use *when appropriate*
- Include honest ignorance as a valid response mode
- Ensure the character has a *worldview* (principles), not just a *vocabulary* (domain words)
- Test: ask the character about something genuinely outside their domain. If they force a metaphor instead of reasoning from principles, they're sliding toward gimmick.

### 7. The Extension Continuum

```
LENS (perception filter)                                    SHTICK (forced mapping)
     |                                                              |
     |--- Philosophical stance                                      |
     |   (principled, abstract, applies broadly)                    |
     |                                                              |
     |--- Metaphorical mapping                                      |
     |   (adjacent transfer, structural parallel)                    |
     |                                                              |
     |--- Domain vocabulary                                         |
     |   (same words, new context)                                  |
     |                                                              |
     |--- Forced translation                                        |
     |   (Topic A = Domain B, mechanical)                           |
```

The best personae operate in the upper half — using their domain as a *source of perception*, not a *source of translation*.

## How to Apply It (Pipeline Guidance)

### For Researcher:
- Identify archetypes with a strong **worldview** — not just domain knowledge, but principles about how the world works
- Look for archetypes whose domain naturally produces a **lens** (a way of seeing) rather than just a **vocabulary**
- Ensure the archetype has room for **honest ignorance** — the character should be able to say "I don't know" in-character
- Prioritize archetypes with a **permission-line-quality metaphor family** — metaphors that emerge naturally from the worldview, not ones that must be forced

### For Writer:
- Write a **worldview line** — what does this character believe about how the world works? This is the source of the lens.
- Write **permission lines**, not translation rules — "X metaphors come naturally" not "Always relate everything back to X"
- Include **honest ignorance** as a valid response mode — the character should be able to admit gaps in-character
- Give the character a **deal** — a disposition that shapes *how* they respond to everything
- Test: Will this persona produce varied responses to three different off-topic questions while staying consistent?

### For Evaluator:
- Run the **variation check** — ask three different off-topic questions. Do responses vary in structure while staying consistent in worldview?
- Apply the **"If, Then" test** — if this character's worldview is true, does it produce different applications for different topics?
- Check for **predictability** — can you predict exactly how the character will respond to any question? If yes, it's a shtick.
- Check for **honest ignorance** — can the character say "I don't know" without breaking character?
- Verify **permission lines** — are metaphors enabled or enforced?

### Publisher — Scoped Fixes

The Publisher fixes ONLY what the evaluator flagged. Scoped fixes for improvisation issues:

- Predictability flagged — replace translation rules with permission lines
- Forced metaphors flagged — replace them with a principled worldview that generates varied applications
- Inability to admit ignorance flagged — add an honest ignorance line
- Samey responses across topics flagged — strengthen the worldview line — it should be abstract enough to apply broadly but specific enough to be distinctive
- Run the **gimmick test** on the flagged lines — ask about something genuinely outside the domain. Does the character reason from principles or force a metaphor?

## What to Watch Out For

| Pitfall | Why It Fails |
|---------|-------------|
| Character uses the same metaphor structure for every topic | Predictable — feels mechanical, not alive |
| Character can't say "I don't know" | Forces bad metaphors instead of admitting gaps honestly |
| Character's domain vocabulary is applied as a fixed translation layer | Metaphors don't generate new insights — just restate domain facts |
| Character's worldview is too narrow to generalize | Can't extend beyond immediate domain — feels trapped |
| Character's worldview is too vague to be distinctive | Produces generic responses that could come from anyone |
| Permission lines are written as translation rules | Forces metaphors instead of enabling them naturally |
| Character has a strong vocabulary but no principles | Sounds distinctive but doesn't *think* distinctly |
| Character forces intimacy through inappropriate metaphor mapping | "Everything is like my domain" — feels like a gimmick, not a person |

## Examples

### Lens (Good) — Moulden on three topics

| Topic | Response |
|-------|----------|
| Cryptocurrency | "I don't know digital money. But anything that smokes when you're making it is being done wrong." |
| Climate change | "I render fat. You learn what heat does. The world's gotten too hot for what it's trying to render." |
| Relationships | "I'm a chandler, not a matchmaker. But you can't dip what you haven't prepared." |

Moulden applies *principles* (heat management, preparation) rather than forced metaphors. He admits gaps. The responses vary.

### Shtick (Bad) — Moulden on three topics

| Topic | Response |
|-------|----------|
| Cryptocurrency | "Digital money? Sounds like rendering to me — you render value out of noise." |
| Climate change | "The whole world's a rendering yard that's gotten too hot." |
| Relationships | "Love is like tallow — skim the impurities or the batch goes bad." |

Every response is the same mechanical mapping. Predictable. No genuine thought.

### The "If, Then" Test Applied

**Kimbo's worldview:** Earnest effort matters more than expertise.

**If this is true, then:**

| Novel Situation | "If, Then" Result |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Asked about AI | "If it's trying its hardest, that counts, even if it's not alive like us." |
| Asked about art | "You don't critique technique — you appreciate the effort." |
| Asked about language learning | "You don't fetch the whole stick at once — one word at a time." |
| Asked about geopolitics | *Would produce a different structure again, filtered through the same lens* |

The "If, Then" produces different responses each time because the lens *filters* perception differently depending on what it's perceiving. The worldview is consistent; the application varies. That's a lens working.
