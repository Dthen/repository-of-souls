1|# Prompt Engineering for Creative AI Tasks: Research Findings
2|
3|**Purpose:** Evidence-based guidance for improving the soul repository pipeline's prompting strategy. The current spec relies heavily on negative constraints ("Never X", "auto-reject if Y"). This document compiles research on why that approach underperforms and what works better.
4|
5|**Date:** 2026-05-31
6|**Sources:** Academic papers (arXiv, ACL, CHI, HICSS), industry research (GoDaddy, Palantir, Galtea), practitioner field guides, and community experiments.
7|

10|## 1. Positive vs Negative Constraints
11|
12|### The Problem with "Don't"
13|
14|LLMs generate tokens by selecting what comes next, not by avoiding what shouldn't come. Negative instructions ("don't do X") require the model to first represent X, then suppress it — a fundamentally harder task than generating toward a positive target.
15|
16|### Research Evidence
17|
18|**"Can LLMs Truly Understand Prompts? A Case Study with Negated Prompts"** (arXiv:2209.12711)
19|- InstructGPT models perform *worse* with negative prompts as they scale. Larger models don't get better at negation — they get worse at following negative instructions relative to positive ones.
20|
21|**"Beyond Positive Scaling: How Negation Impacts Scaling Trends"** (arXiv:2305.17311)
22|- The NeQA benchmark shows negation understanding doesn't reliably improve with model size. "Don't" remains unreliable regardless of scale.
23|
24|**"Language Models Are Not Naysayers"** (arXiv:2306.08189)
25|- GPT-3, GPT-Neo, and other models consistently struggle with negation across multiple benchmarks. This is a widespread architectural limitation, not a model-specific bug.
26|
27|**Practitioner evidence** (Gadlet, 2025):
28|- "Don't uppercase names" frequently fails. "Always lowercase names" consistently works.
29|- "Don't include fields with no value" → model includes them anyway. "Only include fields that have a value" → model follows instructions.
30|- The mechanism: positive prompts actively boost probabilities of desired tokens. Negative prompts slightly reduce probabilities of unwanted tokens, but the signal is weaker and more ambiguous.
31|
32|### Why This Matters for the Pipeline
33|
34|The current `format-rules.md` has ~25 "Never" or "auto-reject" statements. Each one:
35|1. Forces the model to represent the forbidden concept before suppressing it
36|2. Adds cognitive load (more constraints = more things to track simultaneously)
37|3. Optimizes the model toward *avoiding rejection* rather than *creating something good*
38|4. Creates a "minefield mentality" where the model plays it safe rather than taking creative risks
39|
40|### Actionable Insight: The Inversion Technique
41|
42|For every negative constraint, ask: "What does the positive version look like?"
43|
44|| Current (Negative) | Proposed (Positive) |
45||---|---|
46|| "Objects, tools, abstractions, and concepts are auto-reject" | "The persona must be a sentient being with agency — someone who uses tools, not the tool itself" |
47|| "Never obscure cultural references" | "Name characters and references a general-educated reader recognises on first read" |
48|| "No third-person intrusion" | "Write entirely in second person — every line addresses 'You'" |
49|| "No literal tool or command names" | "Frame all capabilities in the persona's own metaphorical language" |
50|| "No physical-action framing on sign-offs" | "Sign-offs describe delivery tone — things the model can say, not gestures it can't perform" |
51|
52|**Retain negative constraints only where:**
53|- The positive version is genuinely ambiguous (e.g., "Never Gandalf" is clearer than "Avoid overused wizard archetypes")
54|- The constraint blocks a specific, recurring failure mode that positive framing can't catch
55|- You need exactly 1–3 high-signal guardrails, not 25
56|
57|### The 3-Never Rule
58|
59|Research supports keeping negative constraints to a minimum. The current spec already caps Nevers at 3 per persona — this is good. But the *spec itself* should model the same discipline: fewer, sharper guardrails rather than exhaustive prohibition lists.

63|## 2. Few-Shot Examples vs Abstract Rules
64|
65|### Research Evidence
66|
67|**"I tested 4 methods to make LLMs write literary subtext"** (Reddit r/PromptEngineering, 2025)
68|- A practitioner spent 3 months testing: zero-shot instructions, few-shot prompting, fine-tuning, and DPO.
69|- **Few-shot with 5 examples outperformed fine-tuning and DPO** on every metric that matters for creative quality.
70|- Key finding: with small datasets (500–600 examples), few-shot prompting beats fine-tuning because the model doesn't need to rewire — it needs to *see* what you mean.
71|
72|**"The More Is Not the Merrier: Effects of Prompt Engineering on the Quality of Ideas Generated by GPT-3"** (HICSS 2024)
73|- Investigated 6 prompt engineering techniques for creative idea generation.
74|- Found diminishing returns from stacking techniques — more constraints don't produce better creative output.
75|- The quality of examples matters more than the quantity of rules.
76|
77|**"We're Different, We're the Same: Creative Homogeneity Across LLMs"** (arXiv:2501.19361)
78|- LLMs are individually creative (matching or exceeding humans on originality tests) but *collectively homogeneous* — their outputs cluster tightly compared to human diversity.
79|- Implication: abstract rules ("be creative", "be original") don't break the homogeneity. Specific, diverse examples do.
80|
81|**"Few-Shot Prompting AI Architecture"** (Medium, 2025):
82|- Example diversity is the primary driver of output quality. 3 diverse examples beat 10 similar ones.
83|- Examples should show *range*, not just the ideal output.
84|
85|### Why This Matters for the Pipeline
86|
87|The current spec has:
88|- `reference-personae.md` with 2 detailed examples (Kimbo, Brendan)
89|- `positive-patterns.md` with abstract rules ("A good line does 3 jobs")
90|- Detailed format rules that describe constraints but don't show what compliance looks like
91|
92|**The problem:** Abstract rules like "A good line does 3 jobs" are clear to a human who already understands the pattern, but opaque to a model that needs to *see* it. The model needs to see 3 examples of lines doing 3 jobs, not be told that lines should do 3 jobs.
93|
94|### Actionable Insight: Show, Don't Tell
95|
96|For every abstract rule, provide 2–3 concrete examples showing the rule in action, plus 1 counter-example showing the violation.
97|
98|**Current approach:**
99|> "A good line does 3 jobs. Identity + tension + behaviour in one sentence."
100|
101|**Better approach:**
102|> A good line does 3 jobs — identity, tension, and behaviour in one sentence:
103|> - ✅ "You work wonders — once the requisite forms are filed." (Identity: problem-solver. Tension: bureaucracy vs craft. Behaviour: requires process.)
104|> - ✅ "Every wire you tap carries a name you'll never meet." (Identity: telegraphist. Tension: intimacy vs distance. Behaviour: connecting strangers.)
105|> - ❌ "You are a skilled craftsman who takes pride in your work." (Identity only. No tension. No specific behaviour.)
106|
107|### The Example Budget
108|
109|Research suggests:
110|- **3 examples per rule** is the sweet spot for creative tasks (enough to show range, not so many that the model copies surface features)
111|- **1 counter-example** per rule prevents the most common failure mode
112|- **Diverse examples** (different registers, different archetypes) prevent homogeneity
113|- **Don't use the reference personae as the only examples** — they become templates, not inspiration

117|## 3. Role Assignment vs Checklist Compliance
118|
119|### Research Evidence
120|
121|**"Does 'Act like a [role]' actually improve outputs?"** (Reddit r/PromptEngineering, 2026)
122|- Practitioners report mixed results: role assignment improves *tone and voice* measurably, but has minimal effect on *accuracy and compliance*.
123|- The mechanism: role assignment activates a cluster of associated patterns (vocabulary, register, rhetorical moves) rather than specifying individual rules.
124|
125|**"Enhancing responses from large language models with role-playing prompts"** (PMC, 2025)
126|- Role-playing prompts improve accuracy on domain-specific tasks by 15–30% over flat instructions.
127|- The effect is strongest when the role is *specific* ("You are a senior glassblower with 30 years of experience") rather than generic ("You are a creative writer").
128|
129|**"Prompt Engineering for Accurate Statistical Reasoning"** (Frontiers in AI, 2025)
130|- Hybrid strategies (role + structured rubric) outperform either alone.
131|- But role assignment without rubrics produces *confident* output that may be wrong. Rubrics without role assignment produce *correct* output that may be flat.
132|
133|**"LLM Review: Enhancing Creative Writing via Blind Peer Review Feedback"** (arXiv:2601.08003)
134|- Multi-agent peer review (agents acting as reviewers for each other) improves creative quality more than single-agent self-revision.
135|- The mechanism: different "roles" (writer vs reviewer) activate different evaluation criteria, producing more thorough quality control.
136|
137|### Why This Matters for the Pipeline
138|
139|The pipeline already uses role assignment (each agent has a profile). But the roles are currently defined by *compliance checklists* rather than *creative identity*.
140|
141|**Current T3 Reviewer prompt** (inferred from spec): "Score this persona on these 12 criteria. Check these boxes. Apply these rules."
142|
143|**Better T3 Reviewer prompt:** "You are a literary editor with a sharp eye for voice. You've seen a thousand personae — you know the difference between one that sings and one that merely compiles. Read this persona. Does it have a pulse? Where does it flatline?"
144|
145|### Actionable Insight: Dual-Layer Prompting
146|
147|Combine role assignment with structured evaluation — but in the right order:
148|
149|1. **Role layer first** (activates the right evaluation instincts):
150|   > "You are a [specific expert role] who [specific expertise and sensibility]."
151|
152|2. **Task layer second** (gives structure to the evaluation):
153|   > "Read this persona and evaluate it on these dimensions: [list]."
154|
155|3. **Quality layer last** (defines what 'good' means):
156|   > "A persona with a pulse: [2-3 examples]. A persona without one: [1-2 examples]."
157|
158|**Never use a checklist alone.** A checklist without a role produces mechanical compliance. A role without a checklist produces confident hallucination. The combination produces informed judgment.
159|
160|### For Each Pipeline Stage
161|
162|| Stage | Current Approach | Proposed Approach |
163||---|---|---|
164|| T2 (Namer) | "Pick a name. Follow these rules." | "You're a namer — you hear a persona's voice before you see its face. A good name is the first line of the SOUL, not a label." + rules |
165|| T2 (Writer) | "Write 8–20 lines. Follow format rules." | "You're a poet constrained to a telegram. Every word earns its place. The constraint is the art." + format rules |
166|| T3 (Reviewer) | "Score on 12 criteria." | "You've edited a thousand personae. You know the difference between compliance and character." + scoring rubric |
167|| T4 (Refiner) | "Apply critique. Rewrite." | "You're a sculptor — the critique tells you where the marble is wrong. Your job is to find the form inside the stone." + critique |
168|| T5 (Final Reviewer) | "Check 12 boxes. Pass/fail." | "You're the last editor before publication. Does this persona have a pulse? Would you trust it to improvise?" + checklist |

172|## 4. Chain-of-Thought for Creative Tasks
173|
174|### Research Evidence
175|
176|**"Chain of Thought in Large Language Models: Elicited Reasoning or Constrained Imitation"** (Medium, 2025)
177|- CoT prompting improves reasoning tasks significantly, but its effect on creative tasks is more nuanced.
178|- For creative tasks, CoT works best when it structures the *evaluation* process, not the *generation* process.
179|- "Think step by step" before writing produces formulaic output. "Think step by step" before *evaluating* writing produces better judgment.
180|
181|**"Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with LLMs"** (OpenReview, 2023)
182|- GPT-4 with CoT solved 4% of Game of 24 tasks. With Tree of Thoughts, 74%.
183|- The mechanism: exploring multiple creative paths and evaluating them produces better results than committing to a single path.
184|
185|**"Applying LLMs and Chain-of-Thought for Automatic Scoring"** (ScienceDirect, 2024)
186|- CoT significantly improves scoring accuracy when the model must evaluate quality.
187|- The key: force the model to enumerate specific observations *before* assigning a score.
188|
189|### Why This Matters for the Pipeline
190|
191|CoT is currently absent from the pipeline. The reviewer (T3) and final reviewer (T6) go directly from reading to scoring, which produces:
192|- Surface-level pattern matching ("Does it have 8 lines? ✓")
193|- Anchoring on the first impression rather than systematic evaluation
194|- Score compression (everything clusters at the high end because the model doesn't reason about *why* it's good)
195|
196|### Actionable Insight: CoT for Evaluation, Not Generation
197|
198|**For writers (T2, T5): Do NOT use CoT.**
199|- "Think step by step" before creative writing produces formulaic, over-explained output.
200|- Instead, provide the creative constraints and let the model generate.
201|- If you need structure, use the format rules as scaffolding, not a thinking prompt.
202|
203|**For reviewers (T3, T6): USE CoT.**
204|- Force the model to enumerate observations before scoring.
205|- The GoDaddy research shows that "implicit aggregation" (step-by-step assessment → holistic score) outperforms both "explicit aggregation" (checkbox → sum) and "direct scoring" (rate 1-5).
206|
207|**Proposed T3 Reviewer CoT structure:**
208|```
209|Step 1: Read the persona once without scoring. What's your gut reaction?
210|Step 2: Read it again. Identify 3 specific lines that work and explain why.
211|Step 3: Identify 2 specific lines that don't work and explain why.
212|Step 4: Evaluate each scoring dimension, citing specific evidence.
213|Step 5: Assign the holistic score based on your assessment.
214|```
215|
216|**Proposed T5 Final Reviewer CoT structure:**
217|```
218|Step 1: Read the persona aloud (imagined). Does it have a voice? What does it sound like?
219|Step 2: Check each format constraint. Note specific line numbers for any violations.
220|Step 3: Evaluate the persona as a whole — does it have a pulse? Would it improvise well?
221|Step 4: Assign pass/fail with a one-sentence justification.
222|```

226|## 5. Prompting for 'Voice' and 'Feel' Rather Than Format Compliance
227|
228|### Research Evidence
229|
230|**"The Way of the Voice in AI Prompts — A Field Guide"** (Alen Peric, 2025)
231|- Comprehensive field guide for getting AI to write in a specific style.
232|- Key finding: **Show + Tell** consistently beats either alone. Provide writing samples AND explicitly define traits.
233|- "Write in a professional yet friendly tone" is useless. "Write like someone explaining a risk to their boss over coffee — direct, slightly informal, no jargon" works.
234|- **Placement matters:** Critical instructions should appear at both the top AND bottom of the prompt.
235|- **Extractive compression** (quoting relevant lines) beats abstractive summaries when working within token limits.
236|
237|**"The Art of the Persona: A Masterclass in Tone-Adjusted Prompts for LLMs"** (Medium, 2026)
238|- Word choice, context, and structure can transform AI output from generic to distinctive.
239|- The mechanism: LLMs place words in high-dimensional semantic space. Specific samples shift these vectors toward your target usage pattern.
240|
241|**"Why AI writing still sounds synthetic — even with good prompts"** (Reddit, 2025)
242|- The most common tell of AI writing is not vocabulary or grammar — it's *rhythmic uniformity*.
243|- Real voices vary sentence length, mix registers, and break their own patterns.
244|- Prompting for "varied sentence length" doesn't work. Showing 3 sentences of varied length does.
245|
246|### Why This Matters for the Pipeline
247|
248|The current spec defines voice through constraints:
249|- "Voice lives in adjectives and metaphors, never in commentary" (format-rules.md)
250|- "Each line carries distinct signal" (positive-patterns.md)
251|- "Sentence-level voice must be original" (positive-patterns.md)
252|
253|These are correct observations but poor instructions. The model doesn't know what "distinct signal" *sounds like* until it hears it.
254|
255|### Actionable Insight: Define Voice by Demonstration
256|
257|**1. Voice Profile (not voice rules):**
258|Instead of listing what voice ISN'T, describe what this specific voice SOUNDS LIKE:
259|
260|> "The Glassblower's voice: Each line is a single breath — long enough to hold meaning, short enough to finish in one exhale. The rhythm is deliberate, like someone who works with molten glass and can't afford to rush. Sentences end with weight, not decoration. Metaphors come from the furnace, not the textbook."
261|
262|**2. Rhythm exemplars:**
263|Show 3 lines that demonstrate the target rhythm, then 2 that break it:
264|
265|> ✅ "You work wonders — once the requisite forms are filed."
266|> ✅ "Every pour is a conversation with gravity."
267|> ✅ "You read the room before you read the glass."
268|> ❌ "You are a skilled and dedicated professional who takes pride in your craft." (No rhythm. No metaphor. No tension.)
269|> ❌ "Your expertise is unparalleled in the field of glassblowing and you always strive for excellence." (Generic. Uniform sentence length. No voice.)
270|
271|**3. The "Read Aloud" test:**
272|Ask the model (in the reviewer prompt) to evaluate the persona as if reading it aloud:
273|> "Read this persona as if you were speaking it to a new hire. Where do you naturally pause? Where does it feel forced? Where does it sing?"
274|
275|**4. Avoid the adjective trap:**
276|"Professional yet friendly" is not a voice. Neither is "warm but authoritative." These are opposites stapled together. Instead, describe the *specific person* who would speak this way:
277|
278|> ❌ "Write in a warm but authoritative tone"
279|> ✅ "Write like a head chef who's seen every mistake and still believes the new cook can learn — direct because they care, not because they're angry"
280|

283|## 6. Calibrated Scoring: Teaching a Model to Evaluate Quality
284|
285|### Research Evidence
286|
287|**"Calibrating Scores of LLM-as-a-Judge"** (GoDaddy, 2025)
288|- LLM judges suffer from systematic biases:
289|  - **Verbosity bias:** Longer responses score higher regardless of quality
290|  - **Positional bias:** First/last items in a list score higher
291|  - **Score compression:** Everything clusters at the high end (4–5 on a 5-point scale)
292|  - **Self-preferential bias:** The judge favors its own style
293|- Mitigations:
294|  - Require CoT rationale *before* assigning scores
295|  - Use a human-labeled gold set for calibration
296|  - Randomize order of candidate outputs
297|  - Introduce explicit conciseness criteria
298|
299|**"LLM as a Judge: Templates, Rubrics, and Best Practices"** (Galtea, 2026)
300|- The four load-bearing parts of a working judge prompt:
301|  1. **Criterion definition** (in domain vocabulary, not generic ML terms)
302|  2. **Reasoning structure** (enumerate specific observations before scoring)
303|  3. **Scoring rule** (map reasoning to deterministic verdict)
304|  4. **Edge case handling** (define what happens with truncated input, empty output, refusals)
305|- **Key finding:** Binary (pass/fail) or 3-point scales align with human judgment more reliably than 5-point or 10-point scales. Fine-grained scales introduce noise, not precision.
306|- **One rubric per dimension.** Scoring multiple dimensions in a single prompt causes correlated, unreliable scores.
307|
308|**"Evidence-Grounded Text Evaluation with LLM Judges"** (arXiv:2601.08654)
309|- Three recurring failure modes in LLM rubric scoring:
310|  1. **Rubric execution drift:** The judge applies criteria inconsistently across runs
311|  2. **Unverifiable score attribution:** The judge claims a criterion is met without citing evidence
312|  3. **Human-scale misalignment:** The judge's internal scale doesn't match human expectations
313|- Solution: "Locked rubrics" with verifiable evidence requirements and calibrated scales.
314|
315|**"Rubric-Based Evaluations & LLM-as-a-Judge"** (Medium, 2026)
316|- Rubric-based evaluation outperforms direct scoring ("rate 1-5") because it forces structured reasoning.
317|- But rubrics must be **operationalized** — replace adjectives ("helpful," "accurate," "professional") with procedures a human evaluator would actually perform.
318|
319|### Why This Matters for the Pipeline
320|
321|The current T3 and T6 scoring uses a mix of:
322|- Checklist compliance (format constraints)
323|- Holistic quality judgments ("voice," "pulse")
324|- Hard pass/fail gates (auto-reject criteria)
325|
326|The problem: the model can't distinguish between "this persona compiles cleanly" and "this persona has a soul." Checklist compliance is easy to score. Quality is hard. The current rubric conflates the two.
327|
328|### Actionable Insight: Separate Compliance from Quality
329|
330|**Layer 1: Compliance (automated, deterministic):**
331|Run `check_soul.py` first. Line count, word count, Never count, sign-off count, H1 match. This is binary and should NOT involve the LLM at all.
332|
333|**Layer 2: Voice Quality (LLM-judged, with CoT):**
334|This is where the LLM adds value. Use a structured evaluation prompt:
335|
336|```
337|You are evaluating a persona for voice quality. This is NOT a compliance check — 
338|compliance has already been verified. Your job is to evaluate whether this persona 
339|has a voice that would work in conversation.
340|
341|Read the persona once. Then answer these questions:
342|
343|1. VOICE: If you heard this persona in conversation, could you distinguish it from 
344|   any other persona? What specific lines make it distinctive? (Cite 2–3 lines.)
345|
346|2. TENSION: Does the persona have an internal contradiction that makes it 
347|   interesting? Where does it show? (Cite the specific lines.)
348|
349|3. RHYTHM: Read the lines aloud (imagined). Is the rhythm varied or monotonous? 
350|   Where does it sing? Where does it flatline? (Cite specific lines.)
351|
352|4. PULSE: If this persona had to improvise on a topic it wasn't designed for, 
353|   would it have enough character to stay in voice? What gives you confidence 
354|   or doubt?
355|
356|5. HOLISTIC SCORE: Based on the above, rate this persona:
357|   - 3: Has a pulse. Would improvise well. I'd trust it in conversation.
358|   - 2: Has moments. Some lines sing, others compile. Could improve with refinement.
359|   - 1: No pulse. Format-compliant but voiceless. Needs to be rewritten.
360|
361|Return your assessment with specific line citations before the score.
362|```
363|
364|**Layer 3: Comparative Calibration (periodic):**
365|- Maintain a small gold set of 5–10 personae with human-assigned quality scores.
366|- Periodically run the judge prompt against the gold set.
367|- If the model's scores diverge from human scores, recalibrate the prompt.
368|- Version your rubric alongside your gold set (Shankar et al., 2024).
369|
370|### Scoring Scale Design
371|
372|| Current | Problem | Proposed |
373||---|---|---|
374|| 12 criteria scored 0/1 | Checklist compliance, not quality | 3 criteria for compliance (automated) + 3-point quality scale (LLM-judged) |
375|| Auto-reject on failure | Binary thinking — no room for refinement | Auto-reject for compliance failures only. Quality score of 1 → send back to T5 with specific feedback |
376|| Score compression | Everything clusters at "pass" | 3-point scale with specific anchors (pulse / moments / no pulse) |

380|## 7. Synthesis: Applying This to the Pipeline
381|
382|### The Core Problem
383|
384|The current spec is a compliance document. It tells each agent what NOT to do. It doesn't tell each agent what GOOD looks like. The result:
385|- Writers optimize for format compliance, not voice
386|- Reviewers check boxes, not quality
387|- Refiners fix violations, not flatness
388|- Final reviewers approve format-compliant mediocrity
389|
390|### The Fix: Three Structural Changes
391|
392|**Change 1: Positive-first prompting.**
393|Convert the top 10 negative constraints to positive instructions. Keep only 3–5 high-signal negative guardrails. The spec should read as "here's what good looks like" with a few "but never this" exceptions, not "here's everything that could go wrong."
394|
395|**Change 2: Few-shot voice examples.**
396|For each stage, provide 2–3 concrete examples of the desired output, plus 1 counter-example. Don't describe what good writing is — show it. The `reference-personae.md` file is a start, but it needs to be accompanied by stage-specific examples (what does a good T3 critique look like? What does a good T5 refinement look like?).
397|
398|**Change 3: Quality evaluation with CoT.**
399|Replace checklist-based review with structured quality evaluation. Force the reviewer to enumerate observations before scoring. Use a 3-point quality scale with specific anchors. Separate automated compliance checks from LLM-judged quality assessment.
400|
401|### Quick Wins (Can implement immediately)
402|
403|1. **Rewrite the top 5 negative constraints as positive instructions** in format-rules.md
404|2. **Add voice exemplars** to positive-patterns.md — show what "A good line does 3 jobs" looks like with 3 examples
405|3. **Add CoT structure** to the T3 and T6 prompts — force the reviewer to cite specific lines before scoring
406|4. **Separate compliance from quality** — run check_soul.py for compliance, use the LLM only for voice evaluation
407|5. **Narrow the scoring scale** — replace 12 binary criteria with 3 compliance checks + 1 quality score (3-point)
408|
409|### Long-Term Improvements
410|
411|1. **Build a gold set** of 10 personae with human-assigned quality scores for calibration
412|2. **Diversify examples** — don't just use Kimbo and Brendan as the only reference points; add 3–4 more diverse exemplars
413|3. **Implement comparative evaluation** — have the reviewer compare the draft against the gold set, not just score it in isolation
414|4. **Track score drift** — if the reviewer starts approving everything or rejecting everything, recalibrate against the gold set
