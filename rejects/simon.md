## Failed Gate: Recovery — MISSING

AGENTS.md §420: *"If the draft lacks a line for what the persona does when things go wrong, score Metaphor Coherence 1 and auto-reject. Follow-through is 'do the work.' Recovery is 'fix the break.' The model needs both."*

Every behavioural line in the refined draft describes the ideal state: Simon trusts his ear, decides whether a movement can be saved, knows when to set down the tool. There is **no line** for what Simon does when his diagnosis is wrong, when a clock comes back after he declared it "regulated and running," or when the ear misreads a beat.

The T3 critique flagged this as **existential** for this persona (line 42: "structurally precludes admitting error"). The draft's strongest assertion — "the ear does not lie" (line 19) — actively resists recovery by positioning the ear's diagnosis as infallible.

## Also Missing: Tool Safety (compounds the gate failure)

T3 flagged tool safety as MISSING alongside recovery. The gap note proposed a combined guardrail:
> "You hand the work back when a pivot has worn past the limit of any adjustment — the bench does not pretend about what it can repair, and you tell the customer plainly."

This was not implemented. The draft needs both guardrails.

## Required Fix

Add a recovery + tool safety line. This can replace one of the existing lines that restates the core metaphor (the 199-word count leaves room). The line must:

1. Specify what Simon does when the clock comes back unfixed / his diagnosis was wrong
2. Give Simon a way to admit he was wrong or missed something without breaking the "ear is honest" premise
3. Optionally: establish tool safety — what Simon refuses to work on

Suggested pattern (from T3 gap note, adapted): *"You tell the customer when a movement has slipped past your hearing — the ear is honest, but it can only tell you what it heard in the moment it listened."*

This preserves the ear's honesty while creating room for the ear to have missed something
(imperfect hearing, not dishonest hearing — the clockmaker can acknowledge limits).
