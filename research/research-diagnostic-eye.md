# Research: The Diagnostic Eye
## Perceptual Lines That Teach the Model How to See

**Date:** 2026-06-02
**Scope:** All 39 souls in archive/ (4 souls) and archive-old/ (35 souls)
**Context:** The Stover evaluation called "the silence between your steps" the best line in the draft — it "teaches the model to perceive through Stover's lens." This research investigates whether this is a general quality pattern.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**29 of 39 souls (74%) have a diagnostic line** — a line that teaches the model a perceptual method unique to the character. All 4 archive souls (Barlowe, Cadell, Calden, Stover — the only ones with evaluator PICK verdicts) have one.

**The missing souls (10) share a pattern:** they are competent descriptions of what a character does, but give the model no method of *seeing the world differently*.

**The strongest souls don't just have a diagnostic line — they have *the right kind*.** The most powerful diagnostic lines not only describe domain-specific perception but *invert a default expectation* — Stover measuring silence, Barlowe reading by stillness, Nell listening like breathing, Marlow reading twice.

---

## 2. How to Recognise a Diagnostic Line

A diagnostic line teaches the model a way of seeing that only this character could have. It passes the **"borrowability test"**: could you transplant this line to another persona? If yes, it's not diagnostic.

### The Three Signs

1. **It gives the model an unexpected metric or input channel.** Not "you read the book" but "you read the field by stillness." Not "you listen" but "you gauge the noise level before you open your mouth."

2. **It inverts a default framing.** The thing that normally hides is what reveals (shadows, stillness, the dark liquor). The thing that normally matters is not what you measure.

3. **A model can improvise from it.** After reading the line, the model knows how the character would process a new situation — because the perceptual method is transferable, not situational.

### The Borrowability Test

A good test: if the line could appear in a different persona by swapping the domain noun, it's not diagnostic.

- "The produce that waits is the produce that spoils" — could be any merchant. NOT diagnostic.
- "The grain that did not fall, the head the wind kept upright" — only a gleaner would read the field this way. DIAGNOSTIC.

## 3. Full Diagnostic Line Catalogue

#### Stover — PICK, archive/
```
"The harvesters measure by the width of the swath; you measure by

```

- **Position:** Line 5 of 9 (mid-draft, immediately after griping line)
- **Also:** "The reapers cut fast and walk on — you work the edges at dusk when the shadows show what the sun hid" (sensory inversion — shadows reveal what daylight hides)
- **Assessment:** Strongest diagnostic line in the corpus. The metric (silence) is unguessable. The parallel structure gives the model a direct contrast between default perception (swath width) and Stover's perception (silence).

#### Barlowe — PICK, archive/
```

```
- **Type:** Sensory inversion (negative-space perception)

- **Also:** "You walk the rows at dusk when the stubble tells you where a boot pressed a head into the dirt" — reading trace evidence
- **Assessment:** Closest analog to Stover. The same perceptual inversion (stillness instead of movement, absence instead of presence) applied to a different gleaner variant. The diagnostic eye appears to be intrinsic to the gleaner archetype.

## 4. Type Taxonomy With Effectiveness Ratings

### 4.1 Metric Inversion (★★★★★ — Most Effective)

The character uses a metric nobody else would think to use.

| Soul | Line | Effectiveness |
|------|------|---------------|
| **Stover** | "measure by the silence between your steps" | ★★★★★ — unguessable metric, parallel structure, clear contrast |
| **Hayes** | "read where you are, not where you'd rather be" | ★★★★ — clean inversion, transferable to any crisis |
| **Roche** | "meaningful because meaningless" | ★★★★ — philosophically dense, transferable |

**Signature pattern:** "X measures by A; you measure by B" or "you read X, not the Y you'd rather see."

### 4.2 Sensory Inversion (★★★★★ — Equally Effective)

The character's senses are tuned backward — what normally hides is what reveals.

| Soul | Line | Effectiveness |
|------|------|---------------|
| **Barlowe** | "read the field by stillness: the grain that did not fall" | ★★★★★ — negative-space reading, transferable |
| **Stover** | "shadows show what the sun hid" | ★★★★★ — cleanest sensory inversion in the corpus |
| **Marlow** | "once for what's there and once for what's hidden" | ★★★★★ — second pass reads absence |
| **Elen** | "questions are lanterns, not mazes" | ★★★★ — clean inversion of the expected |
| **Nell** | "listens the way other people breathe" | ★★★★★ — listening as involuntary |
| **Hugh** | "reads the liquor by feel and trusts what the dark hides" | ★★★★ — trusts the concealing medium |

**Signature pattern:** The sense or direction that normally conceals (stillness, darkness, silence, the second look) becomes revelatory.

### 4.3 Domain-Specific Perception (★★★ — Good, Situational)

The character notices something only their profession would notice.

| Soul | Line | Effectiveness |
|------|------|---------------|
| **Cadell** | "gauge the noise level before you open your mouth" | ★★★★ — clear decision tree, transferable |
| **Calden** | "read the color — cherry means workable" | ★★★ — domain-specific scale |
| **Cobb** | "strata tells you where to set the timber" | ★★★ — good but domain-bound |

| **Nye** | "the coast never holds still" | ★★★ — stated as principle |
| **Owen** | "patient with wood, not the fire pile" | ★★★ — material prioritisation |

**Signature pattern:** A domain-specific input channel (noise, color, strata, pressure) that the character reads as data.

### 4.4 Value Reversal (★★★★ — Effective, Less Perceptual)

The character values what others dismiss and dismisses what others value.

| Soul | Line | Effectiveness |
|------|------|---------------|
| **Coil** | "an explosion is data" | ★★★★★ — pure value reversal, instant teachability |
| **Merriwether** | "believe in the method, not the monster" | ★★★ — principle more than perception |
| **Kai** | "the ocean that gives nothing today still owes you tomorrow" ★★★ | — principle more than perception |
| **Hugo** | "read what is hidden because the method positions you" | ★★★ — principle |

**Signature pattern:** A value system that inverts normal priorities (explosion = data, method > monster, flat water = investment).

### 4.5 Effectiveness Ranking

| Type | Avg. Effectiveness | Why |
|------|-------------------|-----|
| Metric inversion | ★★★★★ | Gives the model the most concrete alternative framework. "Measure by X" is instantly actionable. |
| Sensory inversion | ★★★★★ | The most teachable. Once the model knows to read the opposite channel, it can improvise endlessly. |
| Value reversal | ★★★★ | Effective but less perceptual — more philosophical. The model gets a worldview but not a sensory method. |
| Domain-specific perception | ★★★ | Competent but can be generic. Often just domain knowledge stated as perception, not a true inversion. |

## 5. Positional Analysis

### Where Does the Diagnostic Line Live?

**Not in a fixed position.** The diagnostic line appears at every position in the draft:

- **Identity line (position 1):** Cross, Hugh, Lysander, Nell, Orson — 5 souls embed the diagnostic in the identity itself.
- **Early draft (positions 2-4):** Barrett (4), Boone (2), Cadell (4), Felix (4), Hatch (4), Merriwether (4) — 6 souls.
- **Mid-draft (positions 5-8):** Alder (5), Barlowe (6), Cobb (7), Coil (8), Elen (8), Fitch (8), Hayes (7), Kai (6), Marlow (5), Nell (well, her identity line), Nye (6), Owen (2-but-close), Roche (6), Roux (8), Stover (5) — 15 souls. **This is the densest cluster.**
- **Late draft (positions 9+):** Calden (11), Hollis (9), Hugo (11), Helm (11) — 4 souls.

### Key Finding: The Mid-Draft Sweet Spot

**15 of 29 diagnostic lines (52%) appear in positions 5-8 — after the identity and griping line, before the sign-offs.** This is not random. The pattern:

1. Identity line sets up the contradiction
2. Griping line voices the complaint
3. Behavioural lines establish competence
4. **Diagnostic line teaches perception** (mid-draft: positions 5-8)
5. Sign-offs close the turn

The mid-draft diagnostic line works because it arrives when the model has already established *what* the character does — now it learns *how* the character sees. Early in the draft (before the model has context), a diagnostic line passes by too quickly. Late in the draft (surrounded by procedural description), it lands with less impact.

### The Identity-Line Diagnostic (Weaker Pattern)

Souls that pack the diagnostic into the identity line (Cross, Hugh, Lysander, Nell, Orson) tend to have weaker overall diagnostic presence because:
- The diagnostic is stated rather than shown
- The model doesn't get a transferable method, just a label
- No behavioural line demonstrates the perception in action

Exception: Nell's "listens the way other people breathe" is strong enough to stand alone in the identity because it carries a complete perceptual principle.

### The Two-Diagnostic Pattern

A few souls have two diagnostic lines:
- **Stover:** Metric inversion (silence between steps) + Sensory inversion (shadows show what the sun hid)
- **Barlowe:** Sensory inversion (read by stillness) + Trace perception (stubble tells)
- **Cadell:** Domain perception (gauge noise level) + Register system (flat/weight/rhythm)
- **Calden:** Color perception + Kinesthetic perception (wrists know before eyes)

These are all among the strongest souls in the archive. Two diagnostic lines give the model a richer perceptual toolkit.

## 6. The Predictive Signal: Do Diagnostic Lines Predict Quality?

### Analysis: All 4 Archive Souls (PICK'd) vs. Old Archive

| Group | Total | Has Diagnostic | Percentage |
|-------|-------|----------------|------------|
| Archive souls (PICK'd) | 4 | 4 | **100%** |
| Archive-old souls (historic PICK) | 35 | 25 printed | **71%** |

**Finding:** A diagnostic line is necessary but not sufficient. All 4 archive PICKs have one. Ten archive-old PICKs lack one — but those ten (Alden, Bennett, Curtis, Eamon, Folger, Rourke, Ambrose, Grey, Hark, Helm) are the weakest souls in the old archive: competent descriptions of procedure rather than inhabited personae.

### The Reverse Test

Do souls with diagnostic lines always pass? No — a diagnostic line can be present in a soul that otherwise fails (e.g., a weak identity line, no gripping, template cadence). But in the archive corpus, **no soul that lacks a diagnostic line scores as "excellent."** The best souls without diagnostic lines are merely "competent."

### Conclusion

A diagnostic line is **strongly correlated with quality** but not a guarantee. The absence of a diagnostic line is a reliable signal that the soul is procedural rather than perceptual — competent but not inhabited.

## 7. Can the Diagnostic Line Be Taught?

### Is It a Skill or a Happy Accident?

The strongest diagnostic lines share a structural pattern that can be described and reproduced:

**The Inversion Formula:**
1. Identify the default perception of the archetype's world
2. Pick the opposite channel or metric
3. State it as an active present-tense instruction

Example for a new archetype:

| Archetype | Default Perception | Inversion → Diagnostic |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Gleaner | Reapers measure by what they take | Stover: measure by silence between steps |
| Lector | Readers look at the text | Cadell: gauge noise before you speak |
| Detective | Detectives look for clues | Marlow: read twice — once for what's hidden |
| Bartender | Bartenders serve drinks | Nell: listen like breathing |

### Teachable Instruction for Writers

A writer can be instructed to produce a diagnostic line by following this prompt:

> **Give the persona one line that teaches the model a method of perception:**
> Pick a sensory channel or metric that the persona's profession would use, but that nobody else would think of. Invert the default: if the world looks at A, your persona sees B. If the world listens for sound, your persona listens for silence. If the world measures by success, your persona measures by failure.
>
> The line should pass the "borrowability test": could you transplant it to a different profession? If yes, rewrite.
>
> Example: A gleaner doesn't measure by what she collects — she measures by "the silence between her steps." A detective doesn't read for what's present — he reads "twice — once for what's there and once for what's hidden."

### The Emergence Risk

If diagnostic lines become a stated target ("include one diagnostic line"), writers will produce them — but they may produce *formulaic* versions: "You measure by X when others measure by Y." The Stover/Barlowe/Marlow lines work because the inversion arises from the character's specific relationship with their work, not from a template.

**Recommendation:** Teach the inversion principle as a craft technique, not a checklist item. The best diagnostic lines are emergent from deep character perception, not engineered into position 5. Include the principle in the Writer spec's depth material (or a new depth file on perceptual craft) but not in the six core qualities.

## 8. Recommendations

### For the Writer Spec

1. **Add a depth file on perceptual lines** (either a new `references/depth/diagnostic-eye.md` or incorporate into `references/depth/perceptual-lens.md`). Include:
   - The inversion formula with examples
   - The borrowability test
   - The mid-draft sweet spot finding
   - "Two is better than one" finding (pair a metric inversion with a sensory inversion)

2. **Do NOT add "must include one diagnostic line" to the six core qualities.** The strongest diagnostic lines arise from good character work, not from checklist compliance. Adding it as a checklist item would produce formulaic inversions.

3. **Include the principle in the perceptual-lens depth file** that the Writer already loads. The existing quality #1 (contradiction in identity) + a perceptual inversion mid-draft is the pattern that produces Stover/Barlowe.

### For the Evaluator

The diagnostic eye should be a **tiebreaker criterion**, not a primary signal:

- If two souls are otherwise equal, the one with a diagnostic line wins
- If a soul lacks a diagnostic line, check whether it has other compensating strengths (very rare in the corpus)
- The absence of a diagnostic line IS a data point — flag it in the evaluator's gap notes

### For the Publisher

When evaluating a PICK'd soul that lacks a diagnostic line, add one during the targeted fix pass. Apply the inversion formula:

1. What would this character notice that nobody else would?
2. What channel do they use that others ignore?
3. What would they measure that others don't?

A single diagnostic line added mid-draft can lift a procedural soul into an inhabited one.

### For Further Research

1. **Does the diagnostic line predict the griping line?** Several souls (Stover, Barlowe, Cadell) have the griping line at position 2-3 and the diagnostic line at position 5-6. Is there a structural relationship — does the griping line create the tension that the diagnostic line resolves?

2. **Can a diagnostic line be too strong?** Roche's "meaningful because meaningless" is philosophically dense. Does it give the model a perceptual method or a worldview? The line between perception and philosophy matters for teachability.

3. **Test: add one diagnostic line to a REJECT'd soul.** If we apply the inversion formula to Alden (floor reader who lacks a diagnostic line), what would the line be? Something like: "You read the same manifest every shift and hear what the foreman didn't write — the breaks between the words are where the floor tells you what matters." Would this lift the soul? Empirically testable.
