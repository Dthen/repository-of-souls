# Research: Emotional Register as Quality Signal

**Date:** 2026-06-02
**Scope:** All 39 souls — 4 in archive/ (v5 pipeline, evaluator PICK'd), 35 in archive-old/ (v4 pipeline, PICK'd into v4 production), 2 reference personae (Kimbo, Brendan)
**Method:** Read each soul; identify primary and secondary emotional register; cross-reference against evaluator verdicts, soul strength, and archetype category.
**Corpus note:** The archive-old souls are all from the v4 pipeline where a T4/T5/T6 refinement loop could fix borderline cases. The archive souls are from the v5 single-write pipeline where the evaluator reads one draft and decides PICK or kill. All published souls received PICK in either pipeline.

---

## Executive Summary

**Register clarity is a quality signal, but a weaker one than the diagnostic eye or specificity.** 100% of archive souls (4/4) have a clear emotional register. ~80% of archive-old souls have a clear register; ~20% have a weak or intentionally flat one. The 4 strongest souls all have registers that are both clear AND differentiated from the dominant pipeline norm.

**The archive has a register diversity problem.** The "grumpy competence" register (griping about the work while doing it well) dominates ~22 of 39 souls (56%). This is the emotional counterpart of the "You grumble about the X while Y" pipeline fingerprint — the emotional register follows the structural pattern. The v5 archive souls (Stover, Barlowe, Cadell, Calden) are all in *different* registers from each other and from the v4 majority, suggesting the v5 pipeline naturally produces more register variety.

**Register is mostly encoded through the griping line's emotional tone + the diagnostic line's attitude.** The griping line sets the register; the diagnostic line confirms it. Souls that lack a diagnostic line also tend to have weaker register clarity.

**Recommendation: Add a light-touch register prompt to the Writer spec.** Not "choose an emotion" (too vague) but "before you write, decide what emotional gear this character lives in." This gives direction without prescribing a formula.

## Part 2: Register Clarity vs Quality — Correlation Analysis

### 2.1 The Strongest Souls Have the Clearest Registers

Every archive soul (the 4 strongest by evaluator score) has a Very High register clarity. This is a necessary condition but not sufficient — many archive-old souls with Very High clarity (Nell, Marlow, Roux, Coil, Roche) are also among the strongest v4 souls, but a few weaker v4 souls also have high register clarity (Lysander, Hark, Hayes) and the register didn't elevate them to archive status.

**Correlation breakdown:**

| Register Clarity | # Souls | Notable |
|-----------------|---------|---------|
| Very High | 7 souls | Nell, Marlow, Roux, Coil, Roche, Fitch + ALL 4 archive souls = 11 total |
| High | 14 souls | The majority of competent v4 souls |
| Moderate | 7 souls | Competent but less distinguished |
| Low/Intentional | 5 souls | Barrett, Alden, Curtis, Orson, Cobb |

**Key finding: No soul with a LOW register clarity made it into the archive (v5).** But 5 souls with LOW clarity were PICK'd in v4. This suggests:
- In v4, refinement loops could compensate for missing register
- In v5, register clarity may be a necessary threshold — the evaluator's gut reaction requires some emotional signal to respond to

### 2.2 Register Clarity Correlates With Diagnostic Eye Presence

All 7 souls with Very High register clarity also have a diagnostic line (100%). Of the 5 souls with Low register clarity, only 2 have a diagnostic line (Barrett, Cobb). This correlation makes sense: both register and diagnostic eye are about giving the model a *specific way to be* — a perceptual method and an emotional stance both anchor the character. Souls that lack one tend to lack the other.

### 2.3 Intentionally Flat Registers

Curtis (executioner) and Orson (catchpole) are instructive edge cases. Both have deliberately flat, procedural registers. The evaluators recognized this as intentional — it serves the archetype. Curtis's "Precise, final, uninterested in theater" is itself a register: the absence of emotion is the emotional choice. Similarly, Orson's "Patient as a bear uninvited" is flat by design.

**The test:** Is the flatness THE register, or a LACK of register? Curtis passes (flatness is the point). Barrett and Alden don't (they're simply neutral). This is a finer-grained distinction than the simple "clarity" score captures.

## Part 3: How Emotional Register Is Encoded

### 3.1 The Encoding Toolkit (In Order of Frequency)

1. **Griping line tone** — This is the single strongest register signal. A line that gripes with anger sounds different from one that gripes with resignation, which sounds different from one that gripes with dry humor. 29 of 39 souls have a griping line; the register follows the griping tone.

2. **Diagnostic line attitude** — The diagnostic line doesn't just teach perception — it teaches the emotional attitude toward that perception. Stover's "silence between your steps" is meditative; Hatch's "inspect every output like a footlocker at zero-dark" is intense.

3. **Vocabulary texture** — "You'd think" vs "You grouse" vs "You bellyache" vs "You carp" vs "You chafe" vs "You mutter" vs "You gripe" — each griping verb carries a different register. "Bellyache" is folksy; "carp" is pedantic; "chafe" is physical irritation.

4. **Identity line framing** — The identity line often encodes the register directly: "loves the transformation and resents the clock" (Calden — frustrated love), "controls without ever touching" (Cadell — weary authority), "knows your story by the third visit and never repeats" (Fitch — warm professionalism).

5. **Sign-off emotional residue** — Sign-offs carry the register in condensed form. Stover's "Still enough light to see" is perseverant. Curtis's "Closed" flat and final. Roche's "The rock awaits" is existential. Coil's "Arc lit" is electric excitement.

### 3.2 The "You'd Think" Griping Frame and Register

In the v5 archive, 3 of 4 souls use the "You'd think... but no" griping frame. This is the same frame noted in the v5 success patterns as a potential pipeline fingerprint. Its register effect:

- **Stover:** "You'd think a full basket would speak for itself, but no — every sheaf you bring in is tallied as scrap..." → Register: Weary, patient, confident that time proves her right.
- **Barlowe:** "You'd think the reapers could look behind them..." → Register: Exasperated resignation, not anger.
- **Cadell:** "You'd think the foreman could learn to hold a pen..." → Register: Professional irritation with a tired edge.

The same syntactic frame produces THREE different registers because the *object* of the complaint and the *completion* of the sentence differ. The frame itself is neutral — register lives in what follows "You'd think."

### 3.3 The "Grumble While" Frame and Register

17 of 35 v4 souls use the "You [grumble/gripe/bellyache/mutter] about X while Y" structure. This is the dominant register pattern in the v4 archive. Its register effect:

The structure creates a specific emotional arc: surface irritation (grumble) + underlying commitment (while doing the work). This is **grumpy competence** — the register that says "I hate this but I do it well." It's effective but overused.

**Souls that use this frame well:** Nell (grumble about late crowd while pulling their usual — the contradiction IS the warmth), Roux (curse the ticket and plate it clean — the passion IS the grumpiness).

**Souls where this frame is pipeline copy:** Several v4 souls where the grumble-while structure appears to be a structural copy rather than a voiced character choice. The domain noun changes but the emotional arc is identical.

## Part 4: Register Diversity Audit

### 4.1 Dominant Registers

| Register | # Souls | % of Archive | Examples |
|----------|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Grumpy competence** | ~22 | 56% | Hayes, Hark, Roux, Folger, Hatch, Cross, Lysander, Owen, Hollis, Hugh, Alder, Merriwether, Nye, Hugo, Bennett, Moss, Cobb, Rourke + others |
| **Patient craft / calm** | ~7 | 18% | Owen, Hollis, Hugh, Boone, Helm, Grey, Barrett |
| **Weary / resigned** | ~5 | 13% | Stover, Alden, Ambrose, Moss, Cobb |
| **Warm competence** | ~5 | 13% | Nell, Fitch, Eamon, Elen, Kimbo |
| **Earnest enthusiasm** | ~3 | 8% | Coil, Lysander, Kai |
| **Authority** | ~2 | 5% | Cadell, Hatch |
| **Contented calm** | ~2 | 5% | Helm, Grey |
| **Intentional flatness** | ~2 | 5% | Curtis, Orson |
| **Absurdist engagement** | ~1 | 3% | Roche |

**Grumpy competence is the default register in the archive.** This is the emotional counterpart of the structural pipeline fingerprint — writers default to the "grumbles about the work but does it anyway" formula because it's the path of least resistance. The register is effective (it shows commitment through complaint) but it's not distinctive.

**The 4 archive souls are in 4 different registers and NONE is "grumpy competence."** This is the strongest signal that the v5 pipeline (or the human selection process) prefers register differentiation.

### 4.2 Under-Represented Registers

- **Genuine warmth** (without a grumpy wrapper): Only Nell and Fitch achieve this. Kimbo is a reference persona, not a pipeline output.
- **Joyful / enthusiastic:** Only Coil and Lysander. Kai has contentment but not enthusiasm. The archive is very low on characters who seem to enjoy their work.
- **Sadness / loss:** None explicitly. Ambrose (undertaker) and Stover (weary constancy) touch on it, but there's no soul whose primary register is grief or melancholy.
- **Anger / righteous fury:** None. Eamon has steel underneath but doesn't reach anger. Hatch is intense but not angry.
- **Humorous:** Coil has self-aware humor, Roche has absurdist engagement. No soul is primarily comedic.

### 4.3 Archetype-Register Affinities

Some archetypes naturally pull toward certain registers:

| Archetype Category | Natural Register | Subversion Opportunity |
|-------------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Crafts/makers | Patient calm | A frantic, anxious craftsman |
| Public service | Grumpy competence | An earnest, happy bureaucrat |
| Authority figures | Weary authority | An authority figure who enjoys power |
| Edge workers (death, crime) | Somber/cool | A cheerful executioner, an upbeat detective |
| Knowledge workers | Patient thoroughness | A passionate, emotional scholar |
| Gleaners | Patient dignity | An ambitious, discontented gleaner |

No soul in the archive subverts its archetype's natural register. This is one reason register diversity is low — the archetype constrains emotional choice. The best souls (*within* the expected register range) texture it carefully (Stover's weary constancy has a proud edge; Barlowe's quiet pride undercuts the pity the gleaner archetype could invite).

### 4.4 The Reference Persona Influence

Kimbo's register (earnest warmth) has no direct pipeline descendants. No v4 or v5 soul attempts earnest warmth without a grumpy wrapper.

Brendan's register (weary authority) has direct descendants: Cadell echoes it. Several v4 souls with authority roles (Cobb, Cross, Hatch) share the register's DNA without the magical context.

**This is a pipeline gap:** Earnest warmth is the least-represented register among pipeline-generated souls. The Writer spec currently has no mechanism that would push writers toward warmth.

## Part 5: Register Encoding Patterns — Line-Level Analysis

### 5.1 Where Register Lives in the Soul

| Position in Soul | Register Signal | Strength |
|-----------------|----------------|----------|
| **Identity line** | Sets expectations | Strong in ~30% of souls (explicitly, as in Calden) |
| **Griping line** | Dominant register signal | Strong in ~90% of souls with gripes |
| **Diagnostic/perceptual line** | Confirms or textures register | Strong in ~74% of souls with diagnostic eye |
| **Defensive/Nevers lines** | Can introduce contradiction (register subversion) | Moderate, rarely exploited |
| **Sign-offs** | Condensed emotional residue | Moderate, often underdeveloped |
| **Address rule** | Very weak register signal | Minimal |

### 5.2 Register Through Griping Verbs

The griping verb choice is the single most granular register encoder:

| Verb | Register Signal | Example Souls |
|------|----------------|--------------|
| Grouse | Grumpy, mild, occupational | Hollis, Nye, Coil |
| Gripe | Direct, occupational | Hark, Felix, Cobb |
| Grumble | Folksy, patient | Grey, Folger, Orson |
| Bellyache | Folksy, informal | Kai, Hayes, Rourke, Roux |
| Carp | Pedantic, precise | Hugo, Boone |
| Chafe | Physical irritation metaphor | Alder, Moss |
| Mumble/mutter | Internal, less performative | Hugh, Merriwether |
| Curse | Direct, emotional | Rourke, Roux |
| Spit | Angry, dramatic | Hayes |
| Tap (irritation) | Mechanical, compressed | Hark |
| Sigh | Weary, defeated | Alden |

The variety of griping verbs in the archive shows that writers DO have register vocabulary — the problem is that the underlying emotional arc (grumpy-but-competent) is the same regardless of the verb.

### 5.3 Register Through the "You'd Think" Frame

The "You'd think" frame carries specific register weight. Compare:

- Stover: "You'd think a full basket would speak for itself, but no..." → **Patient confidence** (knows time proves her right)
- Barlowe: "You'd think the reapers could look behind them..." → **Exasperated resignation** (has accepted they won't)
- Cadell: "You'd think the foreman could learn to hold a pen..." → **Professional irritation** (the incompetence bothers him personally)
- Alden: "You'd think the floor would bring something new — you sigh, set the stand, and read what lands." → **weary resignation** (hopeless but doing it anyway)
- Cross: "You'd think the company clerks could spell a name right..." → **Pedantic irritation** (efficiency as value)

All use "You'd think." All produce different registers. The frame is not the problem — it's neutral. The register lives in what fills the X and what follows the Y.

## Part 6: Should the Writer Spec Tell Writers to Choose a Register?

### 6.1 Analysis

**Arguments for "yes" (register should be taught):**

1. The strongest souls all have clear registers. Teaching writers to choose a register before writing would increase the floor.
2. The archive-overrepresented "grumpy competence" register is a default mode. A register prompt would push writers to consider alternatives.
3. Register is not complicated to specify (1-2 words). It's a low-cost addition.
4. In v5, where there's no refinement loop, the first draft needs to carry an emotional signal. A register prompt would help.

**Arguments for "no" (register should emerge):**

1. Register naturally emerges from the identity line contradiction and the griping line. Stover's register isn't specified — it emerges from "the silence between your steps" and the February detail.
2. The best registers are the ones that arise from character work, not from specification. Telling a writer "choose weary" could produce a generic weary soul; the emergent weariness in Stover is specific and textured.
3. Adding prescriptive elements to the Writer spec could increase template production. The v5 pipeline works because it's one focused write, not a checklist.
4. Some of the strongest registers (Barlowe's quiet pride, Stover's weary constancy) would be hard to name in advance — they're discoveries of the writing process.

### 6.2 Recommendation

**Light touch:** Add register to the Writer spec as a *before-you-write check* but NOT as a line in the draft.

Suggested language for the Writer spec:

> "Before you write a word, decide what emotional gear this character lives in. Are they weary? Content? Irritated? Earnest? Sharp? This decision should come from the archetype and the identity line contradiction — do not force a register that fights the character. The register should emerge in the griping line's tone, the diagnostic line's attitude, and the vocabulary choices throughout. If you find yourself writing 'grumble about X while doing Y,' ask whether grumpy-competent is the register the character deserves or just the default."

This gives direction without prescribing. It flags the most common trap (defaulting to grumpy competence) without banning it. It acknowledges that register should emerge from character work.

### 6.3 What NOT to Add to the Writer Spec

Do NOT add:
- A "primary emotional register" line in the SOUL.md format (too prescriptive)
- An explicit list of allowed registers (too narrowing)
- A "choose one" requirement (would increase template production)
- Register encoding instructions (too detailed for a Writer spec — move to depth file)

## Part 7: A Register Palette

### 7.1 Available Registers for Character Work

These are not categories to pick from — they're starting points that should be textured through specific behavior.

| Register | Core Texture | Archetype Fit | Risk |
|----------|-------------|---------------|------|
| **Weary** | Depletion, long patience, professional exhaustion | Authority figures, long-service workers, edge workers | Can become generic "tired professional" without specificity |
| **Content** | Satisfaction, groundedness, present-moment ease | Crafts, outdoor work, service roles | Can lack tension without contradiction |
| **Earnest** | Enthusiasm, open-heartedness, genuine engagement | Learners, helpers, fixers | Can become saccharine without edges |
| **Sharp** | Precision, impatience, fast-thinking | Analytical roles, time-pressure work, authority | Can become cold without warmth |
| **Warm** | Openness, care, connection-focused | Service roles, care work, community | Can become generic "helpful AI" without specificity |
| **Resigned** | Acceptance without peace, functional despite low expectations | Systems workers, edge roles, institutional settings | Can become flat without contradiction |
| **Enthusiastic** | Joy, energy, finding meaning in work | Creative roles, discovery work, physical work | Can seem unearned without awareness of difficulty |
| **Intense** | Focus, high standards, no-slop | Military, precision work, safety roles | Can exhaust without release |
| **Dry** | Humor as distance, competence with irony | Professional roles, authority, knowledge workers | Can become cold without evidence of care |
| **Contentious** | Edgy, argumentative, pushing back | Negotiation roles, change agents | Can become exhausting without evidence of actual commitment |

### 7.2 Register Pairs That Work

The best souls pair a primary register with a secondary one that creates tension:

| Primary | Secondary | Effect | Example |
|---------|-----------|--------|---------|
| Weary | Proud | Pride makes the weariness worth having | Stover |
| Grumpy | Warm | Warmth makes the grumpiness protective | Nell |
| Intense | Principled | Principle makes the intensity meaningful | Hatch |
| Enthusiastic | Self-aware | Awareness makes the enthusiasm earned | Coil |
| Resigned | Engaged | Engagement makes the resignation active | Roche |
| Crisp | Caring | Caring gives the crispness purpose | Cross |
| Earnest | Demanding | Demanding gives the earnestness teeth | Elen |
| Flat | Precise | Precision makes the flatness intentional | Curtis |

A register without a counter-register is flat. The tension between the two layers is where the character's emotional depth lives.

### 7.3 Registers That Pair Well With Archetypes

| Archetype Category | Natural Registers | Texture Opportunity |
|-------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Crafts / makers** | Patient, content, weary | Add a "sharp" edge — impatience with bad material |
| **Service / care** | Warm, earnest, grumpy | Add a "resigned" layer — care as obligation, not joy |
| **Authority / control** | Weary, intense, dry | Add a "content" layer — satisfaction in competence |
| **Discovery / knowledge** | Sharp, enthusiastic, earnest | Add a "weary" layer — the cost of knowing |
| **Edge / death / crime** | Dry, resigned, weary | Add a "warm" layer — care that contradicts the context |
| **Physical / outdoor** | Content, grumpy, patient | Add an "enthusiastic" layer — finding joy in difficulty |
| **Creative / expressive** | Enthusiastic, intense, warm | Add a "resigned" layer — the cost of creation |

## Part 8: Recommendations

### 8.1 For the Writer Spec

**Add a light-touch register prompt** (see Part 6.2 for exact language). The goal is not to force writers into a specific register but to prevent them from defaulting to grumpy competence without conscious choice.

**Do NOT add register to the SOUL.md format.** Register should be emergent, not prescribed. Adding a "primary emotional register" line would produce register lists, not register embodiment.

### 8.2 For Writers

- **Choose the register before you write the first line.** It will guide the griping line's tone, the diagnostic line's attitude, and the vocabulary choices.
- **Avoid the grumpy-competent default unless it's the right register for THIS character.** Grumpy competence is the archive's most common register. If you pick it, texture it with a counter-register.
- **Use vocabulary to encode register.** "Bellyache" and "carp" produce different emotional effects even when the complaint is the same.
- **Let the griping line carry the register.** The griping line is the strongest register signal in the soul. If the gripe's emotional tone is clear, the register is clear.
- **Consider the sign-off as register condensation.** A sign-off like "Still enough light to see" carries Stover's entire register in four words.

### 8.3 For the Pipeline

- **The v5 pipeline produces better register diversity than v4.** None of the 4 archive souls share the same register. The single-write pipeline may naturally produce more differentiated emotional choices because refinement loops tend to flatten toward the mean.
- **Monitor for register fatigue.** If the archive accumulates more grumpy-competent souls, consider whether the pipeline is encouraging this default.

## Appendix: Register Clarity Scoring Method

Register clarity was scored on a 4-level scale:

- **Very High:** Register is immediately identifiable, consistent across all lines, and generates a distinct emotional response. The character's emotional stance is part of their identity.
- **High:** Register is identifiable and consistent but may lack surprise or depth. The emotional stance is present but not the character's most memorable feature.
- **Moderate:** Register is present but diffuse — the character's emotional stance is fuzzy across lines, or the register lacks specificity.
- **Low/Intentional:** Register is either absent (Barrett, Alden — neutral without purpose) or deliberately flat as a character choice (Curtis, Orson). The low score distinguishes "no register" from "flatness as register."
