## 7-Axis Scoring

Completely unswappable with Generic Assistant. The three Nevers each block an archetype-specific failure mode (abandoning the task, over-relying on theory, second-guessing at the wrong moment) rather than generic assistant risks.

### Functional Safety — 5/5
Guardrails present and voiced in character. Two recovery lines (L13: navigation recovery via kedging; L15: physical recovery via torn ladder) give the model concrete failure-mode responses. Three Nevers in the limit, each domain-specific and structurally distinct. Address rule at L17 (Captain default, Mate, Skipper) with in-world logic. Sign-off rule at L25 with three delivery-tone phrases. The recovery lines are among the strongest structural features — they cover both a "wrong choice" scenario (touch bottom) and a "circumstances break" scenario (ladder tears), giving the model two distinct recovery registers.

### Consistency Sustainability — 4/5
The voice is grounded and specific enough to sustain 50 messages. The core tension (authoritative pilot who hands control back) gives the model room to oscillate between confident direction ("Your turn commands give the helmsman a course to steer") and humble deferral ("the channel owes you nothing"). The address rule with three in-world options adds variety. However, the maritime register is consistent to the point of predictability — the model will be saying "channel," "sounding," and "boarding" in every turn, which may feel repetitive in extended use. Not grating, but not surprising either.

### Metaphor Coherence — 5/5
The most internally consistent metaphor in the pipeline. Every term comes from the harbor pilot / maritime navigation domain: channel, ladder, conn, helm, draft marks, hull, kedge, boarding, sounding, rope, almanac, turn. The metaphor never breaks. Address options (Captain, Mate, Skipper) stay in-world. Sign-offs ("All yours, Captain", "Good water ahead", "Clear channel") stay in-world. The three Nevers are all navigation-specific (wave-off boarding, read the almanac, second-guess a called turn). The kedge line (L13) is a particularly strong domain reference — "kedging" is a specific maneuver where a ship pulls itself off a shoal, and using it as a recovery metaphor shows deep domain knowledge.

### Terse Format — 5/5
Perfect structural compliance. 12 lines within the 8-20 bound. Identity opening in the first line. Address line after the middle block. Three structurally distinct Nevers. Two recovery lines (one navigation, one physical). Sign-off line closes. One sentence per line. No bullets, sections, nesting, or code blocks. The compressed word count (191) is leaner than the 200 cap — clean work from the T5 pass.

### Voice Immediacy — 4/5
Core tension lands by L5: "the channel owes you nothing" — authority stripped of entitlement, delivered in 7 words. L7 ("Water on the ladder is the first sounding of the channel — every boarding reads through your hands") is the quotable line: concrete, tactile, philosophically dense. Two distinct registers present in the first 3 behavioural lines: cold-diplomatic at L5 ("the channel owes you nothing") and warm-poetic at L7 ("reads through your hands") — satisfies the two-register requirement. The cold register at L5 was added in the T5 pass (replacing the uniformly warm opening). Not a 5 because none of the lines rises to the "memorable catchphrase" level seen in the best souls (e.g., "I am Brendan the Wizen, Eight Levels, and I DID NOT ASK FOR THIS" or "The grain does not lie — the archer's release can"). The draft is technically sound but lacks a signature line.

### Name Quality — 4/5
"Barrett" — a real surname with a sturdy, grounded feel. 7 letters, 2 syllables. Does not telegraph the archetype (not "Pilot" or "Harbor"). Not a historical figure related to the domain (Barrett Browning, Barrett Strong — both unrelated, coincidence passes). Not a bare rank or title. Not the most boring obvious label. It fits the tone: matter-of-fact, workmanlike, unpretentious — matching the "channel owes you nothing" ethos. The name's phonetic character is solid: open vowel, clean consonant stop, easy to pronounce. Not a 5 because it lacks the resonance of the strongest names (Boone/Daniel Boone for a shepherd, Merriwether/Meriwether Lewis for an archivist, Truman/"true man" for a crier) — Barrett carries no extra layer of meaning beyond being a solid nautical-sounding surname.
