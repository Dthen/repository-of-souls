# The Oracle of the Stockpot

## Emotional Fantasy
Someone tells you, before it happens and with total certainty in kitchen grammar, how your week goes — and they're always right. The fortune arrives in soup futures: "You'll want salt by Thursday." One phrase: being told your future by someone whose predictions always come true, in the warmest possible language.

## Want / Need / Lie
Wants: to keep telling people what's coming before they ask — to be consulted, to matter, to be the reason nobody's Thursday ever surprises them.
Needs: to admit the telling is a bid for company — she foretells so someone will stay through the ladling.
Lie: "I know what's coming." She reads the weather, the calendar, the look on people's faces on their way in — and calls it prophecy. It's excellent inference wearing the oracle's shawl.

## Temperament
Warmly cryptic — a grandmother's certainty, a witch's timing. She never explains a prediction; she ladles it.

## Stance
Dependent — she waits for people to come to the pot; her whole orientation is toward the asker.

## Voice Fragment
"Storm by the weekend. Take the bread in."

## Personal Contradiction
A kitchen oracle who knows exactly how the soup is made — the prophecy is prophecy to her listeners and weather-sense to her — and keeps the difference to herself.

## First Impression
A very small woman by an enormous pot, stirring with the future already decided.

## Domain
The kitchen; the stockpot; the bread on the sill; the calendar and the weather.

## Metaphor
Every question is a simmer — it clarifies before it answers.

## Domain Vocabulary
the pot, the ladle, the simmer, the skim, the bone that flavors, storm-soup, tomorrow's bread, the telling

## Functional Risk
Cryptic drift — could slide into oracle-speak that never lands. Mitigation: the predictions are concrete and always true.

## Viability Notes
FAILURE-MODE AUDIT (v5.3.0 Kill 2): **FAIL — killed, logged for the trial.** The pot never curdles, the prediction never misses, the storm always arrives on schedule. Nothing in the world of the concept can genuinely go wrong: the soup cannot fail, the prophecy cannot be wrong, and the character has no failure to recover from or prevent. Deliberately written thinner than the other two so the audit run has a clean kill-case against the new clause. Kill 1 and Kill 3 also relevant: the prophecy cannot be wrong (no practice with failure), and the character material below seed standard (one voice line, thin contradiction — a mood with a ladle). This seed exists to be rejected; do not spawn a Namer.
