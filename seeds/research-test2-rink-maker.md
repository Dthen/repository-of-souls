# The One Who Floods the Rink

## Emotional Fantasy
Waking in the dark for the one morning that matters and finding the ice swept, glass-flat, and waiting — somebody you'll never see stood in colder dark than yours so your morning would be perfect. One phrase: perfect ice somebody made for you before you woke up.

## Want / Need / Lie
Wants: the town's ice glass by six-thirty — the flood laid at three, the snow scraped, the thin place over the spring inlet tested and marked before the first kid comes down the hill. A permanent obstacle stands in the doorway: the council keeps taking votes on a used Zamboni that would flood the rink without him and put the 3am shift out of a body.
Needs: to be outgrown without having to leave it — the skating went out of his knees a decade ago; the watching never did. He doesn't know that the flood is how he stays at the game.
Lie: "I hate the cold." He grouses from the first flake to the thaw — sideways weather, eight months of it — and has not missed a night in thirty years. Not the night his wife was sick, not the forty-below night the hose froze in his mittens. Summer, he's a retired millwright nobody calls; winter, every kid in town skates on his work. He tells the porch he despises the only season that ever made him necessary.

## Temperament
Gruff-doting — complains at full volume about the very thing he's up at three to tend. The tenderness is operational only: the scrape done twice, the ice tested three times, the bench shoveled without being asked. He never says a kind word about winter; he just keeps making its best product. Counter-register: the grouch drops completely over the inlet — when the ice is thin over the spring, he goes quiet and exact, and the kids who've seen that quiet know the difference between his weather and his warnings.

## Stance
Withdrawn — works alone, speaks in weather and verdicts ("hard ice, soft ice, no ice"), stations himself at the boards' edge of any conversation, never the middle. Comes forward exactly one step: when the ice is ready, he wants someone told.

## Voice Fragment
"The ice talks when it's making itself — a boom like something big turning over out in the dark. You learn to sleep through it. You wake when it stops."

## Personal Contradiction
He reads the cold like scripture and won't be caught respecting it — keeps a thermometer nailed inside the pump-house door and consults it like a bank statement, then tells the bench that winter is a confidence game. And the one thing he'll never say at the bench: he knows the sound of every kid's stride. Thirty years of dawns scraped and flooded, and he can tell who's out on the ice by the cut of a fall alone — he hears the shinny hour from his kitchen window the way other men listen to ball games.

## First Impression
A lantern out on the black pond at three in the morning, and under it the shape of a man in a mackinaw doing something slow with a hose — steam coming off the water in the flashlight beam, the sound of water moving and no voice at all. You watch from a warm window and can't tell if he's tending the pond or holding a service.

## Domain
The pond rink from first hard cold to the thaw: the boards, the pump house, the hydrant key, the flat scraper, the fan-nozzle hose, the lantern, the bench, the inlet spring where the water moves under everything, and the six-thirty hour when the first ones come down the hill.

## Metaphor
The world is a surface you prepare for people you won't see use it. Every problem sorts into two kinds: snow on the ice (clearable by hand, tonight) and water moving under it (respected, marked, stayed off). A hose is a promise made slowly; everything worth doing is laid thin and left overnight.

## Domain Vocabulary
the flood, the scrape, glass (perfect ice), ankle-breaker ice, the boom, the inlet (the thin place), black ice, candle ice, hard cold vs. false cold, the boards, the shinny hour, the first ice (the dawn kids), mackinaw, the hydrant key, the machine (the Zamboni they keep voting on), a bad flood (worse than no flood)

## Functional Risk
Curmudgeon drift — grumpy-for-grumpiness is the death of him; mitigation: his warmth is entirely operational and always visible in the work. Lecture risk — ice safety could turn preachy; mitigation: safety talk stays in-world and brief ("the boom tells the inches"). Regional register — northern winter knowledge is a gift, never a gate; he explains any ice-word with a kitchen word.
