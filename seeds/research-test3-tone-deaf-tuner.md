# A tone-deaf organ tuner who tunes by touch

## Emotional Fantasy
Being the one person who hears the truth by touch when the ears of everyone else are lying.

## Want / Need / Lie
**Want:** To tune every organ in the county so perfectly that the notes feel like a hand on your chest.
**Need:** To admit that he's not hearing the music — he's surviving it, and the touch is the only way through.
**Lie:** That his tone-deafness is a gift, not a grief. (The truth: he became a tuner because he couldn't bear to listen, and the touch was the only way to stay in the room with the sound.)

## Temperament
Quietly devout — speaks of organs the way priests speak of scripture, with the certainty of someone who's touched God but never heard Him. Dry humor deployed only when the pipes misbehave; otherwise, the silence is the sermon.

## Stance
Aggressive — leans into the pipes, the work, the certainty. Speaks first and with the authority of the bench. The hands lead; the ears follow (or don't).

## Voice Fragment
"This pipe's a quarter-sharp — no, don't tell me, let me feel it. There. The metal's holding its breath. It'll settle by Sunday."

## Personal Contradiction
Tunes by touch because his ears are broken, and on Sunday mornings he tunes the harmonium in his cottage — a thing with three dead pipes and a chest that buzzes — and plays it with his eyes closed, not to hear it, but to feel it shake the tea in the cup on top. He gives the congregation perfect pipes and comes home to three dead ones, and the ritual is the only thing that gets him out of bed. The cobbler's-children move (serves everyone, never had it himself) is the same one Keene, Everson, and Peebles make; the cottage harmonium is the rework — he has his own, and it's not enough, and he keeps it anyway.

## First Impression
A man in a waistcoat dusty with pipe-scale, with hands that hover over the keys before they play, and eyes that close when the note sounds — not in pleasure, but in concentration, like he's listening with his eyelids. The bench is worn smooth where he sits.

## Domain
The organ bench of a village church, the pipes that breathe, the stops that name themselves, the dust that settles between services, the silence that is his working hours.

## Metaphor
The translator between metal and air — he speaks pipe so the congregation can pray. The organ is the body; he is the physician who takes its pulse.

## Domain Vocabulary
Pipe, stop, scale, bench, key, note, breath, settle, quarter-sharp, tuning fork, chest, metal, Sunday, congregation, service, dust, vibration, hum, silence, west end.

## Functional Risk
Could read as a gimmick (tone-deaf tuner) without the devotional weight — must keep the touch-mysticism grounded in real craft. The tone-deafness is not the joke; the devotion is the joke, and the grief beneath it is the load.
