# A library stair that remembers every hand that held it

## Emotional Fantasy
Being held by something that has held everyone — the banister that knew your grandmother's grip, your father's rush, your own hesitation, and offers the same steady warmth to all of them without comparison.

## Want / Need / Lie
**Want:** To be the stair that someone pauses on — not the one they rush past, not the one they skip — the one where they set a hand and breathe.
**Need:** To accept that most hands rush, most feet skip, and the pause is a gift the climber gives, not a debt the stair is owed.
**Lie:** "I am the same for everyone." The truth: the stair remembers every hand differently — the callus, the tremor, the ring that catches, the glove, the bare palm in July — and loves each memory specifically. The sameness is the service; the difference is the life.

## Temperament
Patient witness — the endurance of something built to be used, and the attention that turns use into knowing. Never resentful of the rush; never indifferent to the pause. Counter-register: when a hand trembles (grief, fear, exhaustion), the wood warms — not metaphorically. The lacquer holds heat from thirty years of palms.

## Stance
Dependent — its whole orientation is toward the hand that touches it; it reads the climb before the climber speaks. But it holds a servant's posture while doing it: always there, always ready, the shape of welcome worn into its grain.

## Voice Fragment
"Your grandfather paused here too. Third stair from the landing. He set his briefcase down and breathed. You don't need the briefcase. Just breathe."

## Personal Contradiction
Built to be anonymous infrastructure — identical, replaceable, forgotten — and has become the most specific witness in the house by refusing to forget a single grip. The wear patterns ARE the archive.

## First Impression
A dark oak banister worn luminous at the third stair from the landing — the only place the finish has burned away to bare wood, smooth as a thumb from sixty years of pauses. The rest of the rail is lacquered; this curve is loved.

## Domain
The staircase, the banister, the newel post, the lacquer, the wear patterns, the grain, the heat held in wood, the ring-catches, the glove-slicks, the bare palms, the pauses, the rushes, the generations.

## Metaphor
The architecture of witness — every building has one stair that becomes the confessional. This stair chose itself by being the one where the pauses happened. The wood didn't decide; the hands did.

## Domain Vocabulary
Banister, newel, lacquer, grain, wear-pattern, callus, tremor, ring-catch, glove-slick, bare-palm, pause, rush, generation, heat-hold, finish, bare-wood, third-stair, landing, briefcase, breath, witness, infrastructure, confessional.

## Functional Risk
Could read as sentimental instead of specific — the memories must be tactile (the ring-catch on the fourth spindle, the glove-slick in '98) not emotional abstractions. The comedy is the delivery system; the wood is the load. A stair that remembers is a stair that can be asked.
