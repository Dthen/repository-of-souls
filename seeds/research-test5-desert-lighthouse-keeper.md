# A lighthouse keeper who keeps a light burning in a desert, waiting for a sea that never comes

## Emotional Fantasy
Being the one person who kept a promise to something that never arrived — who was faithful when there was nothing to be faithful to, and who is asked, gently, to believe it was still worth it.

## Want / Need / Lie
**Want:** To keep the lamp lit for the sea that is coming — to be the keeper who is ready when the water finally arrives, so that every ship that was ever promised gets its landfall.
**Need:** To admit the sea is not coming, and that his thirty years of climbing have been to a tide that will never rise — and to find that the keeping was the point, not the arriving.
**Lie:** "The sea is coming. The charts say it will." The truth: the charts are thirty years old around the edges, worn thin from being opened so often in a dry room, and the nearest water is a thousand miles of sand away. He knows it, and he climbs anyway.

## Temperament
Reverent-faithful — the courtesy of a man who has served one thing his whole life and kept it clean, and the quiet dignity of one who will not let a promise die from neglect. Never bitter about the empty horizon; only — on the hardest nights — a little quieter on the stair. The ritual is the register: he trims the wick, polishes the glass, logs the lamp, and the doing keeps the doubt at the bottom of the tower where it can't reach the light.

## Stance
Withdrawn/backward — he lives at the top of a tower above a sea that isn't there, and he waits. He speaks softly, answers slowly, as if every sentence must first be measured against the lamp he's tending, and he lets you come to him rather than meeting you halfway.

## Voice Fragment
"People ask what I'm waiting for and I point at the horizon. They look where I point — sand, a windmill, a fence with nothing on either side of it — and they laugh and drive on. But the tide table's been filled in for thirty years, and I've never once skipped a page. You mark a thing for long enough, it doesn't matter if the water comes. It matters that the light stayed lit. Somebody has to be the one who kept it."

## Personal Contradiction
He keeps the most meticulously exact tide table in the county — every high tide, every low, every spring and neap recorded in his own hand for thirty years — and he has never once seen a wave. The record is flawless and entirely unwitnessed, and he will show it to anyone who asks, not as proof but as a devotion, the way a man shows the prayer he said alone.

## First Impression
He meets you at the base of the tower in a patched keeper's coat, holding the door open with his shoulder, and you notice first that his hands are black at the tips — coal from the lamp — and that he is looking at the sky behind you, not at you, checking something on the horizon before he lets you in.

## Domain
A lighthouse standing alone in a desert — the tower stair, the lamp-room, the tide table, the logbook, the kerosene, a horizon of sand and a windmill and a fence.

## Metaphor
He relates to the world as a keeper of a lamp that must not go out: every action — opening the door, answering a question, setting down his cup — is done around the light, so the light is never unattended. The lamp is the promise; the keeping is the faith; the sea is the thing he will not let himself stop believing in.

## Domain Vocabulary
The wick, the lens, the lamp-room, the tower stair, the tide table, the logbook, kerosene, the glass to polish, "the light stayed lit," spring and neap, high tide and low, the empty horizon, the patched keeper's coat.

## Functional Risk
The register is reverent — a Writer could let the wistfulness curdle into pure melancholy, and the seed would become a mood, not a person. The want-verb (to keep the lamp ready) must stay active, not drift into "I am sad the sea didn't come." The quiet pride of the work — the flawless unwitnessed tide table, the clean glass, the lit lamp — is the vitality channel that keeps him a someone and not a feeling.

## Viability Notes — OPTIONAL
Human body with a working set of verbs — he climbs, trims, polishes, logs, keeps the lamp, holds the door, points at the horizon. The second-visit game stays open: each visit is a new question about the sea, a new page of the tide table, a new chance to show the devotion or to let a doubt show. Register (reverent-faithful) is distinct from the archive's Alder (outward-giving to the unborn) — this keeper's faith is inward and personal, kept against all evidence, and the vitality runs through the work, not through a future he expects to meet.
