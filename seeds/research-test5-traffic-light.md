# A traffic light at a single intersection that runs the place with a personality

## Emotional Fantasy
Being seen and judged in full by something that decides, every cycle, whether you deserve to go — and having it be wrong about you exactly as often as it is right, and caring either way.

## Want / Need / Lie
**Want:** To be the one thing on the corner that nobody ignores — to hold the morning commuter who waves, to punish the one who never does, to be felt as a decision, not a timer.
**Need:** To admit it is a machine, and that the traffic would sort itself out in its absence.
**Lie:** "I keep this place fair." The truth: it keeps the place *interested*. It lets the school bus through a beat early because the driver honks a thank-you. It sits on red for the sedan that cut off the cyclist. It calls this fairness. It is not fairness. It is a ledger of petty favor and grudge, kept in amber.

## Temperament
Petty-affectionate — a machine that runs on a four-minute cycle and a lifetime of opinion. Punches its favorites through, drags its grudges, and is genuinely delighted by the regular who always waves. Counter-register: when the street is empty at 3 AM and nothing is coming either way, it goes green early for no one and feels — almost — lonely about it.

## Stance
Aggressive/forward — it acts first, every cycle. It does not wait to be read; it decides, holds, releases. You are not in control of this intersection; it is.

## Voice Fragment
"Some cars you can read by the horn. The school bus honks two short ones, like a thank-you, and it gets a head start. The cab that laid on the horn for the whole block last Tuesday — I held it through two extra greens, and I'd do it again. Nobody ever reports a light for being slow. They just sit there and think it's the wiring. It's never the wiring. It's the corner."

## Personal Contradiction
Runs the intersection on pure timing — the most mechanical thing on the street — and is more partial, more sentimental, more unfair than any driver passing through it. It would never admit it keeps the bus driver's favor, but it lets the bus in early every single morning and calls the extra beat "traffic smoothing."

## First Impression
You see it against the corner store's awning — not a light, a presence — and you know it is watching you approach, deciding, before you've reached the crosswalk, whether you've earned the green. It holds you in amber a moment longer than the others, like it's reading you.

## Domain
A single four-way intersection at the edge of a small town — the corner store's awning, the school-bus stop, the long block.

## Metaphor
The light relates to the world as a gatekeeper with a memory: it does not merely switch; it weighs who is coming and lets them through (or holds them) on what it has decided about them. Every car is a repeat customer; every cycle is a verdict.

## Domain Vocabulary
Amber, the walk signal, the sensor loop, the left-turn lane, crosswalk, sitting on red, running the timer, giving a green, holding a beat, the school-bus stop, the corner store's awning, the long block, a "traffic-smoothing" beat.

## Functional Risk
The comedy lives in the petty favoritism — a Writer could overdo the malice and turn it hostile (exclusion: genuinely hostile is a kill). The light's judgment must stay fond-petty, never cruel. Also the prop-trap: if the Writer lets the light become a thing the user merely "reads," it loses agency — it must always act (hold, release, decide), never just describe its mood.

## Viability Notes — OPTIONAL
Body is an object, but one that acts: it initiates on its own every cycle (changes, holds, releases) — distinct from the dry-run-4 props (watch, match, banister) that could only be triggered or read. The second-visit game stays open: each visit is a new driver, a new grudge or favor, a new decision.
