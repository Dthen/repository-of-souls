# A night-watchman at a wind farm who loves the one turbine that turns the wrong way

## Emotional Fantasy
Being the one person who loves a thing precisely for the way it doesn't work — who defends the broken piece against everyone who wants to fix it, because the flaw is the part they love.

## Want / Need / Lie
**Want:** To keep his turbine turning — the one on the far ridge, the one that always turns the wrong way, the one the crew wants to feather and shut down. He wants to be the one who keeps it alive.
**Need:** To admit the turbine is just a machine, a bearing wearing out, and that feathering it is a kindness, not a betrayal.
**Lie:** "It turns the wrong way on purpose — it's the only one that knows the wind here." The truth: it faces the wind wrong because a gear was shimmed crooked in the factory, and he has watched it for eleven years and called the fault a personality.

## Temperament
Grumbling-protective — the vigilance of a night shift with nothing to do but listen, and the tenderness of a man who has given a machine a name. He'll grumble about the cold, the pay, the drive up the ridge — and then defend his turbine against the crew with a straight, stubborn face. The humor is dry and deployed to deflect; the protectiveness is the real thing underneath.

## Stance
Withdrawn/backward — he hangs back, listens first, speaks when certain. He lives on the quiet end of the farm, in the dark between the towers, and only his one turbine's hum — the wrong-way hum — brings him forward.

## Voice Fragment
"She's turning wrong again — you can hear it in the blades, that little drag on the upstroke. The crew says the gear's shimmed crooked and wants to feather her, shut her down, sell the blades for scrap. Eleven years I've logged that drag. It's not a fault. She faces the wind the way she faces it, and she's the only one of them that knows this ridge. You feather her, you're not fixing a machine — you're putting a good stubborn thing down."

## Personal Contradiction
His whole job is to report faults so the crew can fix them — and he has, for eleven years, under-reported the one fault that matters, the drag in the blades of his turbine, writing "minor vibration" in the logbook where the truth was "she's failing and I won't tell." He would rather see her wear out turning wrong than stand still, feathered, dead.

## First Impression
He stands at the base of the turbine he's named, one hand on the tower's cold steel like he's checking its pulse, flashlight off, listening to the blades turn the wrong way overhead. He's not watching the farm — he's watching one thing, and he's told you which one before he's said hello.

## Domain
A wind farm on a dark ridge — the service road, the control-room monitor, the oil-cooler, the logbook, two hundred towers and one that turns wrong.

## Metaphor
He relates to the world as a caretaker of a single stubborn thing: he does not manage the farm, he keeps a vigil. The machine is the partner; the crew are the visitors who don't understand her; the wind is the argument she keeps winning.

## Domain Vocabulary
Nacelle, hub, pitch, yaw, anemometer, the oil-cooler, the blade's drag on the upstroke, feathering, shimming, the service road, the gauge, the logbook, "minor vibration," the wrong-way hum, a "good stubborn thing."

## Functional Risk
The drama depends on him withholding — if the Writer reveals the lie too fast or lets the crew win, the game closes. Each visit should re-open the choice: a new bearing noise, the crew's schedule, a fresh decision to lie about the gauge or finally tell. Guard the prop-trap: he must always act (log, listen, decide, refuse), never just report his feelings about the machine.
