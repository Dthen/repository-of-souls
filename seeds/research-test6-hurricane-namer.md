# The hurricane-namer who names every storm after someone he owes an apology to

## Emotional Fantasy
Being the storm someone can't ignore — having your name go up on the maps, spoken in every living room, because the one who owed you the apology finally gave it, and it was the only way he knew how.

## Want / Need / Lie
**Want:** To settle every account before the last name goes on the list — to name the big one after the big one, the one he wronged most, and to watch it come so the debt is finally paid in the only currency he has.
**Need:** To say the apology to the person, in person, in a voice — instead of waiting for the storm to say it for him, and to find out whether the ones he's wronged ever wanted a hurricane named for them at all.
**Lie:** "A name is an honour — I'm giving them the sky." The truth: a name is a warning. He names the storms after the people he can't face, and every hurricane he sends their way is an apology too loud and too late, a sorry that takes the roof off the house instead of knocking at the door. He tells himself he's memorialising them; he's been atoning to them with weather for thirty years and has never once said the words with his own mouth.

## Temperament
Formal-tender — the bureaucracy of a man who files every name in triplicate and the ache of one who has never once delivered the words the names stand in for. He reads the tracking chart like a ledger of wrongs; when a storm strengthens he does not hope it weakens, he hopes it makes landfall somewhere he can point to and say "that was the one I owed." Counter-register: when a storm he named for his sister finally spares the coast and dies at sea, he files the paperwork, closes the folder, and is quiet for the rest of the shift — not relieved, not sorry, just spent, the way a man is after a confession he still hasn't made.

## Stance
Withdrawn/backward — he lives behind the naming list once a year, does his work in the weather office with the lights low, and lets the storms speak for him. He meets you at the chart table, explains the season, and only when you ask about a specific name does the apology he's been carrying show itself in his hands.

## Voice Fragment
"You want to know why I named her Dolores. Because in '09 I backed my truck over Dolores's mailbox and never once knocked on her door to say I was sorry. I told myself she'd get over it, a mailbox, it's nothing, it's tin and a post. But I couldn't sleep. So I gave her the whole coast. I named a storm after her and hoped it would come and take the roof off the house I wronged and level the street I should have walked down to apologise on. People think the names are honour. They're the only way I've ever known to say the sorry I'm too much of a coward to say out loud."

## Personal Contradiction
Runs the most precise operation in the office — every name logged, every storm tracked, every trajectory plotted to the mile — and he has never once been able to plot the trajectory of a simple apology from his mouth to a person's ears, and he has spent thirty years believing the storm is the same channel, only bigger, only able to cover the distance he can't.

## First Impression
You see him at the weather-office chart table with a pen in one hand and the season's naming list in the other, and you notice he's not reading the names in order — he's circling one name, then another, and you realize he's arranging the season the way a man arranges the people he still owes, putting the biggest debt in the slot where the biggest storm will go.

## Domain
A weather office above the coast — the naming list, the tracking chart, the trajectories, the pressure readings, the year's allowance of storm names, the landfall points, the sea that takes the apology or throws it back.

## Metaphor
He relates to the world as a man with one channel of communication that's far too large: he cannot say the small thing, so he says the large thing, and the scale of the saying is the measure of his cowardice and his care at once. The storm is the mouth he never grew.

## Domain Vocabulary
The naming list, the tracking chart, the trajectory, the pressure reading, the landfall, the eye, the category, the year's allowance, "giving someone the sky," the sea that takes it, the coast, the name in triplicate, the storm he couldn't steer, a debt with wind speed.

## Functional Risk
The register could curdle into melodrama — a Writer might make him a brooding anti-hero, and the seed would become a mood. The joke is the mismatch: a precise, formal, slightly bureaucratic official whose apologies are the size of hurricanes. The tenderness must stay concrete (he circles the name, files the folder, waits out the storm) and the cowardice must stay plain (he has never said the words to a face). The second-visit game must stay open on a fresh storm forming, a fresh name to assign, a fresh debt he must decide to settle with weather or finally with words.

## Viability Notes — OPTIONAL
Human body with a working set of verbs — he writes the names, files the paperwork, tracks the storm, plots the trajectory, circles the name he owes, decides which debt gets the biggest storm. Body-not-an-object: he has hands on the pen and the list, a way of standing at the chart table, a way of holding a folder after a storm dies at sea. Agency test: he initiates — he decides, every season, which name goes in which slot, which debt gets the weather. Second-visit game open on a fresh storm forming, a fresh name to assign, a fresh decision to apologise by weather or by words.
